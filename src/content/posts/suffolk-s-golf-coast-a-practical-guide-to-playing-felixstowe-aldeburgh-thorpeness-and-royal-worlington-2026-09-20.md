Suffolk rarely comes up in the same breath as Norfolk or Scotland when golfers start planning a trip, which is part of what makes it worth the journey. Within a county that has around 33 affiliated clubs, you can find a genuine links running beside a tidal estuary, a heathland-links hybrid that punishes anything loose off the tee, a nine-hole course Bernard Darwin called "The Sacred Nine," and a James Braid design that looks deceptively pleasant until the gorse gets involved. Suffolk's golf coast, combined with a short inland detour, adds up to one of the most varied two or three-night trips in the English game.

Here is what you need to know before you book.

## Felixstowe Ferry Golf Club

Felixstowe Ferry is where a Suffolk golf trip naturally starts. Founded in 1880, it is the fifth-oldest club in England and the county's only true links course, sitting inside an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty with views across the River Deben to the Shotley Peninsula.

The main course is the 18-hole Martello Championship layout, par 72, designed by Henry Cotton and stretching to 6,379 yards. It earned Championship Venue of the Year from England Golf in 2018, which gives you a sense of the standard. There is also a nine-hole pay-and-play layout, the Kingsfleet course, if you want something more relaxed or want to warm up before a round on the main course.

Visitors are welcome on both courses, but you should contact the club in advance rather than just turning up. Felixstowe is roughly 12 miles east of Ipswich off the A14, so it makes a logical first stop if you are driving in from the south or west.

## Aldeburgh Golf Club

About 25 miles up the coast from Felixstowe, Aldeburgh is the course that tends to stop experienced golfers in their tracks. Founded in 1884 and described as England's second-oldest maritime heathland course, the championship layout is a heathland-links hybrid ranked in the UK top 100. It plays as a par 68 off the men's card, has no par fives at all, and the fairways are lined with dense gorse that punishes anything wayward with genuine finality.

Visitors are welcome on weekdays and weekends, but a handicap certificate is required for the championship course, and fourball play is restricted to certain days only. If you are travelling in a group of four, check with the club beforehand to confirm your preferred day works.

Aldeburgh also has a nine-hole River Course, which overlooks the River Alde, has no bunkers, and is more suitable for higher handicappers or those wanting a gentle second round of the day.

## Thorpeness Hotel and Golf Club

Aldeburgh and Thorpeness are roughly 1.5 miles apart, which makes them a natural two-day pairing. Thorpeness was designed by James Braid in 1922, plays to par 70 at approximately 6,474 yards, and is open to the public. It was voted the greenest golf club in Britain at the 2012 STRI Golf Environment Awards, which reflects how well the course is maintained within its heathland setting.

The finishing hole is played in sight of the House in the Clouds, a distinctive local landmark that has become associated with the course. Thorpeness is widely regarded as one of the finest and most demanding courses in East Anglia. Green fees are not listed here as current pricing was not available at time of writing; contact the club directly for up-to-date rates.

## Royal Worlington and Newmarket Golf Club

This one requires a different kind of planning. Royal Worlington sits inland near Mildenhall, about 40 miles from the coast and a reasonable drive from either Aldeburgh or Felixstowe. It is a private club, and visiting it is not simply a matter of booking online.

All visits must be arranged in advance through the Secretary. Three-balls and four-balls are not permitted. You play in twosomes or foursomes only. If you show up expecting anything different, you will be disappointed. The pace of play is expected to be brisk, with 18 holes (two loops of the nine) typically completed in around three hours.

The effort is worth making. Founded in 1893, designed by Tom Dunn with refinements by H.S. Colt, and granted its Royal prefix by Queen Victoria in 1895, Royal Worlington has been ranked in the UK top 100 for years. It is the home club of the Cambridge University golf team. Bernard Darwin's description of it as "The Sacred Nine" has stuck for a reason. Visitor green fees for the equivalent of 18 holes were listed at £120 on weekdays and £140 at weekends as of 2025 data, though confirming current rates with the Secretary when you arrange your visit is sensible.

Smart-casual dress is expected throughout.

## Putting a Trip Together

The simplest structure is two nights on the coast, combining Aldeburgh and Thorpeness on consecutive days with Felixstowe either side. Add a third night, stay somewhere between the coast and Bury St Edmunds, and Royal Worlington becomes achievable without an unreasonable drive.

Suffolk's coast is among the driest parts of the British Isles, which matters when you are choosing between this and somewhere more exposed in the north. Accommodation in Aldeburgh itself covers everything from small hotels to self-catering cottages within walking distance of the first tee.

Four courses, four completely different styles of golf, and the whole thing fits inside a working week if you can manage it. Suffolk deserves more attention than it gets.
