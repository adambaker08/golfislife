Royal Portrush is brilliant. Nobody is here to argue otherwise. Calamity Corner, the rolling Dunluce Links, the whole theatre of a course that hosted The Open in 2019 and again in 2025. It is a genuine bucket-list round. But at £420 per person for the Dunluce Links between April and October, with tee times that disappear well ahead of the season and a Wednesday closure for visitors, plenty of people arrive on the Causeway Coast and find they need a Plan B.

The good news is that Plan B, and Plan C, are genuinely excellent.

Northern Ireland's Causeway Coast courses deserve to be treated as destinations in their own right, not consolation prizes. Portstewart Strand, Castlerock Mussenden, and Ardglass on the southeast coast are all courses that golfers in the know come back for repeatedly. Here is why they should be on your itinerary regardless of whether you land a Portrush tee time.

## Portstewart Strand: The One That Matches Portrush for Drama

Portstewart Golf Club's Strand Course sits just a few miles along the coast from Portrush and is, depending on who you ask, every bit its equal. The opening holes climb through towering sand dunes and the views across the Atlantic are about as dramatic as links golf gets. It has a reputation among serious links players that has grown steadily over the past decade.

Green fees at Portstewart change from season to season, and no confirmed 2026 rate was available at the time of writing. Check directly with the club before planning around a specific figure. What is consistent in every account of the Strand Course is that visitors find it genuinely memorable rather than merely good.

If you are building a Causeway Coast week and can only add one course to a Portrush trip, many would point you here first.

## Castlerock Mussenden: Often Overlooked, Consistently Underpriced

Castlerock is the one that gets quietly skipped by first-time visitors, and that is their loss. Established in 1901 and extended by Ben Sayers of North Berwick in 1908, the Mussenden Links has an unusual geography: it is bounded on one side by the beach, on another by the River Bann, and on a third by the Belfast to Derry railway line. The nearby Mussenden Temple perched on the clifftops gives the course its name and its most distinctive backdrop.

The 2026 green fees are confirmed: £175 in April and October, and £250 for the May to September peak window. Compared to what the flagship courses cost, that is a serious bargain for a well-maintained links with genuine character. Castlerock has picked up recognition as one of the best-value courses in Northern Ireland, and it earns that.

The Bann Course, a nine-holer, is also on site if you want to play a second loop or warm up before a full round.

Castlerock rewards a bit of local knowledge and a willingness to approach it without the same expectations you bring to Portrush. Treat it as its own experience and you will almost certainly come away impressed.

## Ardglass: The Southeast Gem Worth the Drive

Ardglass sits on Northern Ireland's southeast coast, roughly an hour from the Portrush cluster, which makes it a natural pairing with a Royal County Down trip rather than a Causeway Coast day. But it is worth flagging here because it belongs to the same conversation about courses that punch above their profile.

The clubhouse alone is worth a mention: it is housed in a historic fortified building, one of the more unusual settings you will find at any golf club in these islands. The course itself is a cliff-top links, and continual reinvestment from visitor green fees has brought the condition up noticeably in recent years.

The most recently confirmed green fee is £220 for peak season, based on 2025 data. Confirm the current rate directly with the club before booking, as pricing may have been updated for 2026.

If you are already making the journey to Royal County Down, adding Ardglass to the trip turns a great day into a genuinely memorable short break.

## One to Keep an Eye On

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club on the northeast coast has started to appear more regularly in conversations about emerging, lower-cost alternatives. Research on it is thinner, so it is flagged here as one to investigate rather than book without doing your own homework first.

## Practical Notes for Planning

Most visitors to the Causeway Coast base themselves in Portrush, Portstewart, or Bushmills. All three are short drives from each other, and the courses above are within easy reach. Ardglass is better treated as part of a separate day or overnight on the southeast coast.

The post-2019 Open boom in Northern Ireland golf tourism is real. Tee times at Portrush and Royal County Down require planning months in advance, and green fees at both have more than doubled compared to the pre-2019 era. The courses in this post have seen interest grow too, so booking ahead is still sensible, but you have more flexibility.

## The Actual Takeaway

If you are visiting the Causeway Coast and banking everything on a Portrush tee time, you are taking a risk. If that tee time falls through, or if you simply want more golf around it, Portstewart Strand, Castlerock Mussenden, and Ardglass are not fillers. They are the reason serious links golfers come back to Northern Ireland more than once.
