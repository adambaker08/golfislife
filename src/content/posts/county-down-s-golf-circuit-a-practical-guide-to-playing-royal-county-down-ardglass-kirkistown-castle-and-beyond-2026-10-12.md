## Why County Down, and Why Now

The Open returning to Royal Portrush in 2025 put Northern Ireland back in the spotlight for UK golfers in a way it hasn't been for years. Most of that attention has gone to the Causeway Coast, which is fair enough. But County Down, an hour south of Belfast, has a golf circuit that deserves its own trip entirely, built around one of the most famous courses in the world and backed up by a supporting cast that is genuinely worth the journey.

This guide treats County Down as a practical destination rather than a bucket-list fantasy. Royal County Down is bookable, the fees are steep but knowable, and Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle give you the kind of links golf that makes a three or four-day trip feel properly complete rather than padded out.

---

## Getting There

For UK visitors, the ferry is the most underrated option. Cairnryan in south-west Scotland runs services to Belfast, as does Liverpool, and arriving by sea gives you a gentler entry point than flying. Once in Belfast, Newcastle (County Down) is roughly 45 minutes south on the A24 through the foothills of the Mournes.

If you are flying, Belfast City Airport is 30 to 40 minutes from Newcastle by car. Belfast International adds another 30 minutes or so to that. Either way, hire a car. Public transport will not get you around these courses sensibly.

## Royal County Down: The Reality of Playing It

RCD needs little introduction in terms of reputation. What it needs is practical information, because the booking process and the costs catch a lot of visitors off-guard.

The Championship Course runs to 7,186 yards, par 71, set between the Mourne Mountains and Dundrum Bay. Old Tom Morris laid out the original routing in 1889; Harry Colt refined it later. It has spent years inside the world's top five and has been ranked number one outside the United States by Golf Digest. The Walker Cup came here in 2007, the Irish Open in 2015.

For 2026, visitor green fees on the Championship Course are £450 per person from 20 April to 16 October. That figure is taken directly from the club's enquiry page and is the one number in this post you can rely on. Full payment is required at booking and is non-refundable. Play is in four-ball groups, and a minimum of one caddie per group is required, so budget for that on top of the green fee.

Visitor days are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday. Wednesday and Saturday are members-only. Book well in advance; the course is not quietly sitting there waiting for you.

If your group wants a taste of the place at a fraction of the investment, the Annesley Links (par 66, 4,617 yards, redesigned by Mackenzie and Ebert in 2015) is available to visitors at £120 for the same period. It is a proper course in its own right, not a consolation prize.

For accommodation, the Slieve Donard Resort sits right next to the course and is the obvious high-end choice. The Burrendale Hotel in Newcastle is a mid-range alternative that frequently appears in golf packages combining RCD and Ardglass.

## Ardglass: Clifftop Links, Medieval Clubhouse

About 30 minutes from Newcastle along the south-east County Down coast, Ardglass Golf Club is the kind of place that rewards visitors who bother to look beyond the headline course on any trip.

The clubhouse occupies a medieval castle, which is a genuinely unusual setting. The course itself runs to around 6,268 yards, par 70, with the Irish Sea coming into play on eight holes and visible from all 18. The clifftop positions give some holes a drama that belies the club's relatively low international profile.

Green fees at Ardglass are significantly more accessible than RCD. Sources vary enough that it would be misleading to quote a specific figure here, but checking the club's website directly before you travel is straightforward, and the price point makes it an easy addition to the itinerary rather than another major budget decision.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Ards Peninsula's Best-Kept Secret

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club sits at Cloughey on the Ards Peninsula, on the eastern side of Strangford Lough. It claims the distinction of being the most easterly 18-hole course in Ireland, and it is the nearest links course to Belfast at around 45 minutes by road. Despite all that, it barely registers on the radar of visiting golfers from the mainland.

The club was founded in 1902, and the present layout carries the influence of James Braid, who visited in 1934. The course plays around 6,167 yards, par 69 to 70, on sandy links terrain that drains quickly and stays open even in poor weather. Elevated tees and greens are a recurring feature, which creates some genuine variety in a layout that could otherwise feel compact.

The par-4 10th is generally cited as the standout hole. The indicative green fee figures available online suggest weekday rates starting around £35 and weekend rates slightly higher, but check directly with the club before booking as these can change.

If your itinerary has you playing Ardglass and Kirkistown on consecutive days, the Strangford to Portaferry ferry across Strangford Lough is a useful routing option that cuts the driving time considerably.

## Filling Out the Trip

If your group wants a parkland round for contrast, Warrenpoint Golf Club to the south-west is a par 71 course overlooking Carlingford Lough with the Mournes behind you. It has hosted the Irish Professional Championship and Ladies European Tour events. Green fees are in the accessible range, though again, check the club website for current rates.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

A County Down golf circuit works because the courses are genuinely different from each other and the logistics are manageable. RCD at £450 per round is a considered decision rather than an impulse, but it is one of the few courses in the world that justifies the billing. Ardglass and Kirkistown between them give you two links tests at a combined cost that probably doesn't exceed a single round at RCD, and both are good enough to hold their own on any reasonable itinerary.

Do the ferry crossing, hire a car, and give yourself at least three days. County Down repays the effort.
