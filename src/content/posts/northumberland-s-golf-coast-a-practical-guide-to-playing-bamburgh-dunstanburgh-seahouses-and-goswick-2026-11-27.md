Northumberland's golf coast does not get the attention it deserves. An hour south of East Lothian and its famous names, this stretch of coastline between Embleton Bay and the Scottish border runs through an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty and strings together four proper links courses that most English golfers have never played. The greens fees are, in most cases, a fraction of what you would pay further north. The courses are not consolation prizes either. Two of them rank among the best links tests in the country.

This guide maps the four main stops as a logical driving itinerary, with honest notes on what each course is actually like and the practical details you need before you book.

---

## The Itinerary at a Glance

Starting from Newcastle (roughly an hour south) or Edinburgh (roughly an hour and three-quarters north), the most logical order runs: Seahouses, Dunstanburgh Castle, Bamburgh Castle, then Goswick as the finale up near Berwick-upon-Tweed. Two nights based in Seahouses or Bamburgh puts everything within easy reach. Three nights is comfortable if you want a re-round at Goswick or Dunstanburgh.

---

## Seahouses Golf Club: The Friendly Warm-Up

Seahouses is the right place to start. Founded in 1913 as a nine-hole course and expanded to 18 holes in the 1970s, it sits on the clifftops within the AONB, seven miles from the A1, and plays to a par 67 from under 5,600 yards. It is compact. It is affordable. It is very enjoyable.

The course splits into two distinct characters. The first nine runs inland with water hazards threading through the fairways. The second nine turns coastal, and that is where Seahouses earns its reputation. The 10th requires a tee shot carried entirely over water, and the 15th is played over the cliffs. Neither hole is long. Both demand your full attention.

Green fees for 18 holes run from roughly £22.50 to £44 depending on day and time, making Seahouses the most accessible round on the coast. Visitors are welcome every day but advance booking is essential. The club is GolfMark accredited, dog-friendly throughout, and has a bar and veranda. If you are bringing a less experienced player or simply want a relaxed opening round, Seahouses does the job well.

---

## Dunstanburgh Castle Golf Club: Best Value on the Coast

A short drive south, Dunstanburgh wraps around Embleton Bay on National Trust land and has been a links since 1900. James Braid redesigned it, and his work is still evident in the natural, unforced routing. The Course Rating sits at 71.2, which tells you there is more substance here than the scenery alone suggests.

The signature moment is a par 3 played across a ravine with the ruins of Dunstanburgh Castle sitting on the horizon behind the green. It is a genuinely memorable hole, not just a postcard shot. The rest of the course rewards straight, controlled ball-striking, and the turf is properly links in character.

Dunstanburgh is widely regarded as the best-value round on this stretch of coast. The clubhouse went through a refurbishment completed in late 2025 and early 2026. The course is located on Sea Lane between Craster and Embleton village (NE66 3XQ). If you only have time for two rounds on a weekend trip, Dunstanburgh and Goswick would be a reasonable shortlist.

---

## Bamburgh Castle Golf Club: Scenery First, Golf Second

Bamburgh is the most photographed course on the Northumberland coast, and it earns that status. The views across to Bamburgh Castle (Grade I listed, with origins in the 11th century), out to Lindisfarne, the Farne Islands, and back towards the Cheviots are as good as anything you will find on a golf course in England. Play on a clear morning and it is difficult to concentrate on your scorecard.

Opened in 1904 to a design by George Rochester, the course plays to par 68 at around 5,600 yards. The routing is unusual: two par 3s open the round, followed immediately by two par 5s, which keeps you slightly off-balance throughout. The course is undulating with several blind shots, so reading the course planner before your first visit is worthwhile rather than optional.

There is no pro shop. Logo'd clothing is available from the bar, and hire clubs, trolleys and buggies are on offer. Visitors are welcome every day.

One honest note: opinion divides on whether the green fee represents fair value compared to the other three courses on this trip. Bamburgh wins on atmosphere and scenery, but those who prioritise the quality of the links test over the view tend to prefer Dunstanburgh or Goswick.

---

## Goswick Links: The Proper Test at the Top of the Coast

Five miles south of Berwick-upon-Tweed, Goswick is the standout links of the four. Founded in 1890 and designed by James Braid, with later modifications by Frank Pennink and more recent course improvement work by Mackenzie and Ebert, it stretches to around 6,800 yards at par 72. It has ranked in the top 50 courses in England and the top 100 links in Great Britain and Ireland, and it has served as an R&A Regional Qualifying venue for The Open Championship.

The fescue turf runs firm and fast, as a links should, and both nine-hole loops return to the clubhouse, which makes it a sensible layout whatever the weather throws at you. There are no gimmicks. This is a course that rewards proper links play: low, running approach shots, patience into the wind, and an understanding that the ground is part of the game.

Green fees sit in the higher bracket for the region, with the gap between the cheapest and most expensive tee times being considerable, so check directly with the club before booking. A handicap certificate is required, and advance booking is essential. Visitors are welcome on weekdays and after around 10am at weekends.

If you can only play one course on this stretch, Goswick is the one to build your trip around.

## Practical Notes

**Getting there:** Newcastle and Edinburgh are both sensible bases for a flying visit, but staying on the coast is worth the small extra effort. Bamburgh village, Seahouses, Alnwick, and Berwick-upon-Tweed all work as accommodation hubs, with Seahouses offering the most central position for accessing all four courses.

**When to go:** The courses are playable year-round. Summer tee times book up, so plan ahead. Autumn and spring offer firmer turf conditions and thinner crowds.

**Booking:** All four clubs ask visitors to book in advance. Do not turn up and expect a game, particularly at weekends.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

This is a genuinely underrated golf trip, not in the way that phrase usually gets used as a filler compliment, but in the straightforward sense that most golfers who have played East Lothian have not made the short journey south to play Goswick or Dunstanburgh, and that is their loss. The courses are real, the landscape is extraordinary, and the visitor experience is relaxed in a way that the busier Scottish destinations often are not. Four rounds, two or three nights, and a part of England that the golfing mainstream has not quite caught up with yet.
