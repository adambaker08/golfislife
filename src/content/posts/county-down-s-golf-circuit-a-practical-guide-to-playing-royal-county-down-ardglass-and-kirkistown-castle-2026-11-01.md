If you're planning a golf trip to Northern Ireland and the conversation starts and ends with Royal County Down, you're leaving a genuinely brilliant circuit half-finished. County Down is compact enough to drive across in an hour, but it contains three courses that together make a case for being the most varied and satisfying golf itinerary on the island. This guide maps out that County Down golf circuit practically: how to get there, how to approach each course, and what to expect beyond the famous views.

## Why County Down Rather Than the Causeway Coast

Royal Portrush and the Causeway Coast get enormous attention, and rightly so. But County Down is a separate trip entirely, not a continuation of the same route. The two regions sit on opposite sides of Belfast, they feel different, and they deserve separate consideration. If you've done Portrush, that's not a reason to skip County Down. If you haven't done either, don't try to combine them in three days.

County Down's golfing history runs deep. Royal Belfast Golf Club, founded in 1881, is one of the oldest clubs in Ireland and gives a sense of how long this county has been serious about the game.

## Royal County Down

Newcastle's Championship Links needs little introduction. It consistently appears in the top five courses in the world, set between the Mourne Mountains and Dundrum Bay, with views that stop you mid-backswing if you let them. At 7,186 yards and par 71, the Championship course is a proper test that demands thought as well as distance.

Visitors are welcome but you need to book well in advance, particularly for peak season. Green fees are at the premium end of what you'll pay anywhere in the British Isles. The exact figure varies by season and year, so check the current rates directly at royalcountydown.org before planning your budget.

One practical point worth noting: there are two courses at the club. The Annesley Links (par 66, redesigned in 2015) is shorter and significantly less expensive. If you want a day-before warm-up round, or if someone in your group wants to play within the same setting without the full outlay of the Championship course, the Annesley is a sensible option that's often overlooked.

The Slieve Donard Resort and Spa in Newcastle sits immediately adjacent to the course and is the obvious choice for accommodation if you want to walk to the first tee.

## Ardglass Golf Club

About 30 minutes up the coast from Newcastle, Ardglass occupies a clifftop position on the northeast coast of County Down that would be remarkable even if the golf were unremarkable. It isn't. The course runs to around 6,268 yards at par 70, and the links terrain keeps things honest throughout.

The clubhouse alone is worth knowing about. It's a fortified medieval building dating to around 1405, described as one of the oldest buildings in the world still in use as a golf clubhouse. It sets a tone before you've even reached the first tee.

Ardglass has risen in profile over recent years and green fees have moved accordingly. It's no longer a pure hidden gem, but it remains significantly less expensive than Royal County Down. Check current pricing at ardglassgolfclub.com. Tee times can be booked directly with the club and visitors are genuinely welcome.

The course is regularly cited as the obvious companion to Royal County Down, and that reputation is deserved. It has its own character rather than serving as a consolation prize.

## Kirkistown Castle Golf Club

The third piece of the circuit sits on the Ards Peninsula at Cloughey, about 45 minutes from Belfast by road and reportedly the most easterly 18-hole course in Ireland. Kirkistown Castle was founded in 1902, and James Braid shaped much of the course layout in 1934. That heritage shows in a design that rewards precision and course management over raw power.

Par 70 (or 69, depending on which layout is in play), at around 6,167 yards. The par-4 10th at 435 yards is frequently described as one of the most demanding par fours in Irish golf. The course has an Open Championship-style feel to it, which is partly the terrain and partly the straightforward, strategic demands Braid built in.

One unusual historical footnote: a feature near the first tee known as "The Dip" was created during the construction of a WWII airfield. It's a small detail, but the kind of thing that makes a course feel rooted in actual history rather than marketing narrative.

Kirkistown has been recognised as the best value golf experience in Ulster, and it's worth including on that basis alone. Quick-draining links terrain means the course rarely closes, which matters if you're travelling with fixed dates.

## Getting There from the UK Mainland

Belfast City Airport is the closer option for County Down, roughly 45 minutes by road to Newcastle. Belfast International is further but has broader route coverage from across the UK.

If you'd rather avoid flying, Stena Line runs a ferry from Cairnryan in Scotland to Belfast (around 2 hours 15 minutes), which is a reasonable crossing. There's also a service from Liverpool (Birkenhead) to Belfast, roughly 8 hours, with an overnight option. A hire car is almost essential once you're in County Down. The courses are spread enough that public transport connections won't work well around an early tee time.

## The Practical Takeaway

Three courses, three distinct personalities, all within easy reach of Belfast. Royal County Down is the centrepiece, but Ardglass gives you clifftop links golf with genuine history, and Kirkistown Castle adds a different pace and a precision test that suits a certain kind of golfer more than the famous names do. Plan four days minimum: one for travel, one per course, with a buffer for the weather doing what it does. Check green fees directly with each club before you commit to dates, as pricing at all three has been moving.
