## Angus Is More Than One Famous Name

Most golfers who make the journey north to Angus have Carnoustie in their sights and nothing else. That's understandable. The Championship Course carries enormous weight: eight Open Championships, a reputation for severity that's earned rather than marketed, and a closing stretch that has broken better players than most of us will ever be. But arriving for one round and leaving again misses the point. The Angus coast between Carnoustie and Montrose is one of the most compact, varied, and authentically Scottish links circuits anywhere in the country. You can base yourself in a single spot and play four genuinely different courses without a journey longer than half an hour.

This guide is for visiting UK golfers who want to make a proper trip of it rather than a day-trip pilgrimage to a single famous postcode.

---

## The Circuit at a Glance

The four main courses sit along a tight coastal strip facing the North Sea:

- **Carnoustie Golf Links** (three courses on site: Championship, Burnside, Buddon)
- **Panmure Golf Club** (a few miles along the coast)
- **Monifieth Golf Links** (similarly close, a traditional east-coast layout)
- **Montrose Golf Links** (around twenty miles north, roughly thirty minutes by road)

The whole run from Carnoustie to Montrose is manageable in a single drive. In practice, that means 36-hole days are feasible, course combinations are easy to arrange, and you won't spend your evenings recovering from long transfers.

---

## Carnoustie: Start With the One You Know

If you're going to play the Championship Course, book early. Demand is high in summer, and tee times can go months in advance. Book directly with Carnoustie Golf Links by phone or online. It's worth the planning.

The course itself is described as "Carnasty" for good reason. Over a hundred bunkers, a Barry Burn that crosses the line of play on both the 17th and 18th, and a back nine that offers no shelter from whatever the North Sea decides to send. It isn't conventionally pretty in the way that, say, the Ailsa Course at Turnberry is. It's strategic, demanding, and completely unforgiving of optimism. That's the attraction.

The Burnside and Buddon courses on the same site are often overlooked, but they're worth your attention on their own terms. Green fees are considerably more accessible than the Championship, and Burnside in particular offers a proper test on genuine links turf without the waiting lists or the anxiety. If you're building a multi-day itinerary, opening with a round on one of the secondary courses before tackling the Championship layout the following morning is a sensible way to find your footing.

---

## Panmure: The Understated One With a Famous Story

Panmure Golf Club sits just along from Carnoustie and tends to get a line or two in any Angus itinerary without ever quite getting the attention it deserves. It's an inland links rather than a pure seaside course, tighter and more sheltered, with a character that's quite different from its neighbour.

The historical footnote is well known among golf history enthusiasts: Ben Hogan used Panmure to prepare for the 1953 Open Championship at Carnoustie, which he went on to win. Whether or not that adds anything to your experience of the place is a matter of taste, but it does suggest the course offered something useful to a man who didn't waste time on rounds that weren't.

Visitors are welcome. It's the sort of club where the golf matters more than the marketing, and a round here pairs well with a morning at Carnoustie to make a satisfying day.

---

## Monifieth: No Nonsense East-Coast Links

Monifieth is a few miles from Carnoustie and provides another solid entry in the circuit. Two courses (the Medal and the Ashludie) sit on traditional linksland with the kind of uncomplicated, no-frills atmosphere that east-coast Scottish golf does well. It won't dominate your highlight reel, but it belongs in any serious Angus week and offers a change of pace and character from the courses either side of it.

---

## Montrose: The One That Changes the Conversation

Montrose Golf Links sits around twenty miles north of Carnoustie, which makes it a half-hour run up the A92. It deserves the detour.

Recorded play at Montrose dates to 1562, which places it among the oldest golf venues on earth. The main course, known as the Medal or Championship Course, goes under the name the 1562 Course and offers a full-length links test. A shorter Broomfield Course runs alongside it. Visitors are welcome, and the club's understated manner suits the surroundings: this is golf that has been quietly getting on with things for well over four hundred years without feeling the need to shout about it.

Adding Montrose turns a good Angus trip into an exceptional one.

---

## Where to Base Yourself

Carnoustie town is the practical choice if you want to walk to the first tee and keep things simple. It's modest rather than full of options, so if you want more choice in the evenings, Dundee is around eleven miles away and considerably better served for hotels and restaurants. The city also makes a more comfortable base for those arriving from Edinburgh, which is roughly ninety minutes south by car.

For those thinking about extending the circuit northward, Stonehaven (not far south of Aberdeen) opens up another leg of the journey entirely. But that starts to become a different trip.

---

## A Note on Costs

Green fees across the Angus circuit vary considerably depending on course, season, and whether you're on the Championship layout at Carnoustie or one of the secondary courses. The research available doesn't include current verified figures, so check directly with each club before booking. What's consistent is that, outside of the Carnoustie Championship Course, this part of Scotland represents solid value compared with some of the more aggressively priced Scottish links destinations.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

Angus works as a self-contained golf circuit because the courses are close, varied, and genuine. You're not driving an hour between each one, and you're not playing slight variations on the same theme. Carnoustie's seriousness, Panmure's history, Monifieth's straightforwardness, and Montrose's extraordinary age give the week a shape and a logic. Four days, four courses, one stretch of the North Sea coast. It's a better trip than most golfers realise before they make it.
