## The West of Wales Deserves More of Your Attention

Ask most UK golfers where they'd go for a links trip in Wales and you'll hear Royal Porthcawl, perhaps Aberdovey, possibly a mention of Conwy. Pembrokeshire rarely comes up. That's a genuine oversight. Tucked into the far south-west corner of Wales, inside a National Park that covers some of the most dramatic coastline in Britain, there's a compact cluster of courses that would hold their own on any serious golf itinerary.

This is a practical guide to playing Pembrokeshire's golf courses, covering what each course actually offers, what it doesn't, and how you'd string a two or three night trip together sensibly.

---

## The Anchor: Tenby Golf Club

Any trip to Pembrokeshire starts at Tenby. Established in 1888, it's widely recognised as Wales's oldest affiliated golf club, and that history isn't just trivia. The course has James Braid's fingerprints on it (he extended it from nine to eighteen holes in 1907), Donald Steel carried out later work, and the result is an 18-hole links that plays to a par of around 70 over roughly 6,330 to 6,600 yards depending on the tees.

It sits hard alongside Tenby South Beach, fully exposed to the prevailing south-westerlies that roll in off Carmarthen Bay. The features are proper links: pot bunkers, blind shots off hog's-back fairways, gorse that'll swallow a wayward drive without apology. The views across to Caldey Island make even a bad round easier to accept.

Practically speaking, this is a course that drains well and plays year-round. Visitors are welcome, though a handicap certificate is required and advance booking is sensible. For green fees, check directly with the club rather than relying on any third-party figure online, as prices shift. The club has eleven en-suite twin rooms in an on-site Dormy House, which makes it a natural base.

Golf World ranked Tenby 136th in Britain and Ireland in their 2025 rankings and eighth best in Wales in 2023. That's not marketing; it's a course that stands up to scrutiny.

## Something Different: Newport Links Golf Club

Newport Pembrokeshire is a small town on the north of the county, about ten minutes from Fishguard and sandwiched between the Preseli Mountains and the Irish Sea. Newport Links sits entirely within the National Park, and the setting alone makes it worth the drive from Tenby (roughly an hour).

The original nine holes were laid out by James Braid in 1925, with a second nine added in 2008. The character shifts noticeably between the two halves. The front nine is clifftop and elevated, offering wide views but playing more like a heathland track in feel. The back nine drops lower towards the sea and takes on a more traditional links character. It's an honest hybrid rather than a pure links course, and it's worth going in with that expectation. The variety is part of the appeal.

The course is par 72 and runs to around 6,100 yards. There's on-site accommodation in twin rooms and suites, and the Cat Rock Restaurant at the clubhouse looks out over Newport Bay. Green fees vary by season; check with the club for current rates.

If you're thinking about a golf trip that happens to double as a Pembrokeshire coast holiday, Newport is the option to base yourself at for the northern part of the county.

---

## Worth Half a Day: St David's City Golf Club

St David's is the smallest city in Britain and home to a surprisingly enjoyable 11-hole layout (played with 18 tees, par 70, around 6,116 yards) at The Burrows near Whitesands Bay. It plays in a natural bowl with views over Ramsey Island and St Davids Head that are genuinely spectacular.

This isn't a course to build a trip around on its own, but as an afternoon round on a two or three night visit, it fills the slot perfectly. It's good value, low-key, and drains well. If your travel companion isn't a golfer, they'll find plenty in St David's itself to occupy a morning.

---

## Worth Considering on the Drive In

For golfers travelling into Pembrokeshire from the east, Ashburnham Golf Club in Carmarthenshire sits just over the border. It's one of the older and more demanding links courses in Britain and could work as a first-day stop on the way through. The sweeping views across to the Gower are a bonus.

---

## Getting There and Getting Around

Tenby is on the Pembroke Dock branch line from Cardiff. Direct services take around two and a half to three hours, which makes it genuinely accessible without a car if you're basing yourself in Tenby. Newport Pembrokeshire and St David's require a car unless you're comfortable with infrequent rural buses. Hiring a car in Tenby or Haverfordwest opens up the full county.

Fishguard is fifteen minutes from Newport Links if a ferry crossing to Ireland is part of a longer itinerary.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

Pembrokeshire won't replace a week in East Lothian. But for two or three nights of genuinely good golf inside a National Park, with proper links conditions at Tenby, a contrasting clifftop experience at Newport, and St David's as a bonus, it's a more compelling destination than most UK golfers give it credit for. The crowds aren't there yet. That's the opportunity.
