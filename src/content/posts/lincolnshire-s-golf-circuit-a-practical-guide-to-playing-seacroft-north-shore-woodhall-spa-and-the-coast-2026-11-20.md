## The County Most Golfers Skip (And Shouldn't)

Mention a golf trip to the east of England and most people reach straight for Norfolk: Brancaster, Hunstanton, Sheringham. The conversation rarely makes it another sixty miles north to Lincolnshire. That's a mistake worth correcting.

Lincolnshire's golf circuit, taking in Seacroft, North Shore, and Woodhall Spa, offers something that's genuinely hard to find in one compact trip: two completely different styles of golf, coastal links and inland heathland, within roughly twenty-five miles of each other. Add a world-ranked course into that mix and you have a proper destination. The county doesn't have the marketing machine of East Lothian or the reputation of the Algarve, but that's also why you can still get a tee time without booking six months in advance.

A two- or three-night trip, based somewhere on the Skegness coast, covers all of it comfortably.

---

## Seacroft: The Course That Anchors the Circuit

Seacroft Golf Club sits at the southern edge of Skegness, backing onto Gibraltar Point National Nature Reserve. Founded in 1895 and designed by Willie Fernie, it's an out-and-back links of around 6,492 yards, par 71, running across fast-draining sandy ground alongside the Wash.

It's regularly listed in the Top 100 courses in England, and the course sits on a Site of Special Scientific Interest, so the rough and the margins have a wildlife-reserve quality that most links simply don't have. On a clear day you can see across the Wash to the Norfolk coast. When the wind is up, the return nine will test anyone.

The sandy soil means it's playable year-round, and the Lincolnshire coast is noted for below-average rainfall by English standards, making it a reasonable choice for a shoulder-season trip. Indicative green fees, as noted on the club website and Visit Lincolnshire, run from around £35 midweek in winter to £75 at weekends in summer. Check current rates directly with the club before you book, as these figures change.

Advance booking is essential. Turn up unannounced and you'll likely be turned away.

## North Shore: James Braid by the Dunes

A kilometre from Skegness town centre, North Shore Hotel and Golf Club is the natural base for a coastal day that also includes Seacroft. The two courses are close enough that playing both in a day is realistic if you're organised about tee times.

North Shore was designed by James Braid and opened in 1910. It's par 71, around 6,216 yards, and it blends links and parkland character in the way that courses from that era often do: the dunes are present but it doesn't play like a pure links, and that actually makes it a good contrast to Seacroft rather than a straight repetition. The hotel sits within the course grounds and has thirty-six bedrooms, which makes it the most convenient base on the coastal section of the circuit.

Green fees start from around £30 (again, check current rates directly). The course is a member of the Association of James Braid Courses, which gives you a sense of the design heritage it's working within.

## Woodhall Spa: The One That Changes the Register

Roughly twenty miles inland from the coast, the Hotchkin Course at Woodhall Spa Golf Club is in a different category to most courses you'll play in a lifetime. It was ranked 74th in GOLF magazine's Top 100 Courses in the World in its 2025-26 edition and sits as one of the highest-ranked inland courses in the UK.

The club was founded in 1891, and while the design traces back to Harry Vardon, the course was significantly shaped by H.S. Colt and S.V. Hotchkin. More recently it underwent a restoration by Renaissance Golf Design, with substantial tree felling to regenerate heather and restore the character the course had lost over decades. The result is a proper heathland layout: sand-based, year-round playable, with heather lining every hole and a difficulty that respects handicap limits (men 28, women 36 to play as a visitor).

Woodhall Spa is also home to England Golf and the National Golf Centre, so there's a bit more infrastructure around the place than you'd expect from a village in the Lincolnshire weld. The companion Bracken Course is worth considering if someone in your group wants a different experience, or if you fancy a second round with a more water-influenced layout.

Advance booking is essential here too. This isn't a course you can be casual about.

## Rounding It Out: Sandilands

If your group has mixed abilities, or if someone wants a quieter fourth round without the pressure of a Top 100 course, Sandilands Golf Club at Sutton-on-Sea is worth noting. Founded in 1894, it's Lincolnshire's original links course, sitting directly alongside the beach and offering sea views from the tees. It's flat, accessible, and low-key in the best sense. It won't challenge a single-figure player in the same way, but it rounds out a circuit nicely.

## Getting There and When to Go

Lincoln is the natural gateway, well connected by road and with the A158 running directly east to the coast. The inland drive to Woodhall Spa is around nineteen miles east of Lincoln.

The coastal strip's drier climate makes spring and autumn genuinely viable. Summer weekends book up, but midweek in October or April gives you good conditions with fewer crowds. Winter is possible too, particularly at Seacroft and Woodhall Spa given their sand-based drainage, though always worth confirming course conditions before travelling.

## The Honest Takeaway

Lincolnshire doesn't sell itself, which works in your favour. This is a circuit with a world-class course at its centre, a proper championship links on the coast, and a historic James Braid layout that gives you somewhere to stay and two styles of golf within a short drive of each other. It won't feel like a bucket-list pilgrimage. It'll feel like a good, honest golf trip that you won't have done the same as everyone else.

That's usually the best kind.
