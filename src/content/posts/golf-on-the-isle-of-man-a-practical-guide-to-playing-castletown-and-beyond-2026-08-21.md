## A Links Destination That Nobody Seems to Talk About

The Isle of Man sits in the middle of the Irish Sea, roughly equidistant from England, Scotland, Ireland, and Wales. It is less than 90 minutes by air from most UK airports. It has eight 18-hole courses and one nine-holer on an island 32 miles long and 14 miles wide. And yet, when golfers talk about a links trip, they almost always reach for Dornoch, Lahinch, or Royal County Down before they think of Castletown.

That is, in short, their loss.

This post is for anyone who wants a long weekend of proper links golf without booking a year in advance or spending a fortune on flights to the far north. The Isle of Man is a genuine option, and Castletown Golf Links is the main reason to go.

---

## Castletown Golf Links: Why It Earns the Trip

Castletown sits on the Langness Peninsula, a narrow finger of land in the south of the island with the Irish Sea on three sides. On a clear day, which is not guaranteed, you can see Ireland, Scotland, England, and Wales from the course. On a bad day, you will feel all four of them blowing at you simultaneously.

The pedigree is proper. The original layout was designed by Old Tom Morris in 1892. Alister MacKenzie revised it in 1913. Philip Mackenzie Ross worked on it again after the Second World War, the same man responsible for the post-war restoration of Turnberry. It hosted the PGA Cup in 1979. In 2025 it appeared at 97th in Golf World's ranking of the best courses in Great Britain and Ireland, and it sits in the Rolex World Rankings.

The conditions are what you would hope for: tight-knit fescue fairways that drain quickly, fast greens, gorse and heather lining the holes, and a layout played largely above sea level so the views are almost constant. This is not a course that hides its challenge. The par-4 5th, known as the Road Hole, plays to 423 yards with out-of-bounds tight on the right. The 17th, called the Gully, asks you to drive over a deep ravine with rocks and sea below. These are not manufactured difficulties. They are just the land doing what links land does.

Visitor green fees in high season (May to September) were listed at £120 for 18 holes on where2golf.com in 2025. Treat that as a rough guide and confirm directly with the club before you travel, as pricing changes.

One practical note worth knowing before you arrive: the onsite hotel and clocktower that formed part of the historic Castletown clubhouse were demolished in 2026, and the club is currently operating with a temporary clubhouse. Facilities will be limited compared to what older guides describe. Worth factoring in if you are expecting a grand 19th hole experience.

## The Rest of the Island's Courses

Eight 18-hole courses on an island this size means you are never far from your next round, and the variety is reasonable.

**King Edward Bay**, north of Douglas, was also designed by Tom Morris, laid out in 1893 with an elevated position and sea views. **Rowany Golf Club** in Port Erin on the southwest coast offers coastal views and a more accessible feel. **Peel Golf Club** on the west coast is parkland rather than links, with tight tree-lined fairways that demand accuracy off the tee. **Glen Truan Golf Links** is the newest course on the island and, on a clear day, offers views across the Irish Sea towards Scotland.

**Mount Murray Golf Club** is resort-style and the most family-friendly option, with the island's only floodlit driving range (24 bays). If you need a warm-up round before Castletown or want to bring a non-golfer who still wants a driving range session, Mount Murray fills that role.

Port St Mary's nine-hole course is a curiosity worth a mention: designed by George Duncan, the 1920 Open Champion, it is a pleasant way to fill a spare afternoon.

## Getting There With Your Clubs

**By air:** Ronaldsway Airport is three miles from Castletown, which makes the logistics almost comically convenient. Direct flights operate from London Gatwick, London City, Manchester, Liverpool, Birmingham, Edinburgh, and Bristol, with flight times from the UK mainland around one to one and a half hours. Airlines serving the route include Loganair and easyJet, among others. Check current schedules and bag allowances directly with whichever airline you book, as club carriage policies and fees vary.

**By ferry:** The Isle of Man Steam Packet Company operates services from Liverpool (approximately two hours 45 minutes), Heysham, Belfast, and Dublin. Foot-passenger return fares from the UK have been listed at around £48 on travel sites, though that figure is from an older source and should be verified before you book.

For a multi-course trip, the ferry with a car is the more practical choice. Taking your own bag, moving between courses at your own pace, and not worrying about hire equipment makes the crossing worth considering even if flying is slightly quicker.

**One thing to note on connectivity:** the Isle of Man is a self-governing Crown dependency, not part of the United Kingdom. In practice, UK mobile users generally do not experience roaming issues, but it is worth checking with your network before you travel rather than finding out when you land.

## The Honest Case for Going

Castletown is a course that would draw queues if it sat on the Ayrshire coast or in County Clare. The island around it has enough variety to fill three or four days without ever feeling like you are scraping the barrel. The logistics are straightforward from almost anywhere in the UK. And because most golfers have not thought to go, the tee sheet is not the fight it would be at better-known links destinations.

That combination does not come along often. A long weekend on the Isle of Man is worth organising.
