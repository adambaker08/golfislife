Kent doesn't shout about itself the way Scotland does, but tucked into the east of the county is one of the most concentrated stretches of genuine links golf in England. Royal St George's, Royal Cinque Ports, Prince's, and Littlestone sit within roughly 20 miles of each other on what amounts to Kent's golf coast, and yet most visiting golfers drive down, play Sandwich, tick the box, and leave. That's understandable but it's also a waste. This guide covers the full circuit from Sandwich down to Romney Marsh, with honest notes on visitor access, what each course actually offers, and a few quieter spots worth slotting into the itinerary.

---

## The Big Three Around Sandwich and Deal

### Royal St George's, Sandwich

This is the one everyone wants. A current Open rota venue, par 70, stretching to just over 7,200 yards of proper seaside links with the dunes, the blind shots, and the wind off the Channel that give it its reputation. Visitor access is tightly restricted, tee times fill well in advance, and green fees are firmly in the premium bracket. Your best realistic route in is booking months ahead through the club directly, or going as a member's guest. Turn up hoping for a walk-on and you'll be disappointed.

Worth the effort, though. There's a reason it keeps coming back on the rota.

### Royal Cinque Ports, Deal

A short drive north of Sandwich, Deal is often treated as the consolation prize. It shouldn't be. Royal Cinque Ports is one of the most demanding links in England, and it welcomes visitors on weekdays, which makes it considerably more accessible than its neighbour. It was removed from the Open rota partly due to flooding concerns on its exposed low-lying land, but it served as a Final Qualifying Course as recently as 2025. Championship-level green fees apply, so budget accordingly, but midweek you can get on without the months of advance planning that Sandwich requires.

### Prince's Golf Club, Sandwich

Prince's sits immediately alongside Royal St George's on the same stretch of Sandwich Bay. The 27 holes are laid out across three 9-hole loops (called Dunes, Shore, and Himalayas), and you can play them in various combinations. It has a proper history: 17 of the 27 original greens survived World War Two. It doesn't carry the same name recognition as its neighbour, but it's a serious links course in its own right and visitor access is more straightforward. There's accommodation at The Lodge at Prince's and a restaurant on site (Brasserie on the Bay), which makes it a sensible base if you're planning a multi-day trip rather than a day visit.

## Littlestone: The One Worth the Extra Drive

Littlestone Golf Club, founded in 1888 and sitting between Romney Marsh and the sea near New Romney, is the under-the-radar highlight of this corner of Kent. The original layout came from Laidlaw Purves, with subsequent input from James Braid, Alister MacKenzie, Frank Pennink, Donald Steel, and Peter Alliss at various points across the club's history. That's a remarkable list of names for a course that most visiting golfers don't have on their radar.

The championship layout runs to just over 6,400 yards at par 71, and the bunkering was significantly overhauled in 2021 and 2022. Golf World ranked it among the top 140 courses in Britain and Ireland in its 2026 rankings. Visitors are welcome on weekdays and weekends (Sundays from 11:30), and green fees are competitive for a championship links of this calibre. It's worth calling the club directly for current rates before you book.

The drive from Sandwich takes around 25 minutes. That's not much of an ask for a course this good.

## The Quieter Alternatives Worth Adding

**Walmer and Kingsdown Golf Club** sits on the clifftops near Deal, designed by James Braid. It's shorter and more affordable than the championship venues, the views out over the White Cliffs are genuinely striking, and it makes a good option for a morning round when you want something less intense on the wallet. The clifftop setting gives it a character that's hard to replicate on flatter ground.

**North Foreland Golf Club** is at the northern end of the county near Broadstairs. It blends parkland and links characteristics and offers sea views from parts of the course. Not a pure links, but worth knowing about if you're arriving from that direction or want to extend a Kent trip northward.

**Westgate and Birchington Golf Club**, near Margate, is a short par-64 layout. At under 5,000 yards it's not a serious test for anyone who plays regularly, but it's affordable and useful as a warm-up round early in a trip or as a relaxed option for a mixed-ability group.

## Practical Notes for Planning the Trip

The microclimate around Sandwich and Deal is notably dry by English standards, which makes this corner of Kent usable most of the year. Autumn and early spring can produce excellent conditions with smaller crowds and lower rates.

For bases, Canterbury works well if you want a town with restaurants and hotels and don't mind a 30-minute drive to the courses. Sandwich itself is small but has accommodation options and puts you on the doorstep of the links. For the full Littlestone addition, staying near Deal splits the difference reasonably well.

Midweek is almost always the right call. Better rates, quieter courses, and the visitor access restrictions at some clubs relax slightly outside of weekends.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

Kent's golf coast is worth more than a single-course day trip. If you're making the journey from elsewhere in England, the logical move is three or four days, mixing at least one of the premium Open venues with Deal, Littlestone, and one of the clifftop courses. The courses are close enough together that you're not spending your holiday in the car, and the combination of history, genuine links conditions, and variety across the circuit is hard to match anywhere else in England south of Lancashire.
