County Down is home to what many credible rankers consider the greatest links course in the world. That alone is enough to draw golfers across from the mainland. But if you're flying into Belfast, booking a hire car, and spending two or three nights in the area purely to play Royal County Down once, you're leaving an awful lot of good golf on the table. The county has a coherent circuit of courses, each with real character, and treating them as a region rather than a single destination changes the whole shape of a trip.

This guide covers what the three main stops actually demand of a visitor: how booking works, what it costs in realistic terms, and what kind of golf you're getting into.

---

## Royal County Down: What the Rankings Don't Tell You

Royal County Down's Championship Links has sat at or near the top of Golf Digest and Golf Monthly's world and UK rankings for years. Par 71, set between the Mourne Mountains and Dundrum Bay in Murlough Nature Reserve. Opened 1889, shaped with contributions from Old Tom Morris, Harry Vardon, and Harry Colt at various points. You almost certainly know the reputation.

What the rankings don't spell out is how the club actually works for a visitor.

**Visitor days are limited.** The Championship Links is open to visitors on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday only. Wednesday and Saturday are member days. The Annesley Links (the par-66 second course) is open Monday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday. If your trip falls on a weekend-heavy schedule, you'll need to plan around this.

**There is no ballot.** You submit an enquiry form through the club website, and the Reservations Team comes back to you with availability. The club itself describes tee-time availability as "exceptionally limited," which is an honest warning rather than false modesty. Book as far ahead as you can manage.

**All groups play as four-balls**, and a minimum of one caddie per group is required. A forecaddie costs £110 per group, on top of the green fee. The Championship Links green fee for the 2026 season (20 April to 16 October) is £450 per person. The Annesley Links runs at £120 for the same period. Both fees are paid in full at the time of booking and are non-refundable.

For 2027, fees are already published at £495 and £150 respectively, so it's worth factoring in which season you're planning for.

The blind tee shots on the Championship course are genuinely disorienting the first time. A local caddie earns their fee here, and not just for carrying the bag.

**The Annesley Links** is worth considering in its own right, particularly if the Championship course is fully booked on your preferred dates or if the budget is a constraint. At 4,940 yards from the men's tees, it's shorter and somewhat more manageable in a wind, but it's still links golf with views to match.

## Ardglass: Clifftop Golf with a Genuinely Unusual Clubhouse

About 30 minutes from Newcastle, perched above the Irish Sea on the east coast of County Down, Ardglass Golf Club is the kind of course that would attract a lot more attention if it weren't in the shadow of its neighbour.

Founded in 1896, the par-70 layout runs to 6,268 yards from the white tees. Eight holes have genuine sea influence, and the clifftop exposure means conditions can change quickly. It plays differently on a calm spring morning than it does in a stiff easterly.

The clubhouse partly occupies a fortified building with origins dating back to 1405, believed to be one of the oldest buildings anywhere in the world used as a golf clubhouse. That alone is worth a moment's attention before or after your round.

Visitors are welcome every day at Ardglass, which makes scheduling considerably simpler than Royal County Down. Green fees vary with the season, and given that seasonal variation in the available figures is fairly wide, the most useful thing here is to contact the club directly for current rates rather than rely on a figure that may already be out of date.

Ardglass is roughly an hour from Belfast, which makes it workable as a day trip in either direction.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Underrated One

If Ardglass is underrated, Kirkistown Castle is practically unknown outside Northern Ireland, which is a shame because it's a proper links in its own right.

Located at Cloughey on the Ards Peninsula, it's the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland and sits about 45 minutes from Belfast. The layout was shaped by James Braid, who visited in 1934, and the terrain drains quickly enough that the course is rarely closed after rain. Founded in 1902, par 70 (some sources list it as 69, depending on the tee configuration).

Green fees are notably lower than at Royal County Down or Ardglass. Indicative figures from golf guides suggest weekday rates from around £35 and weekend rates from around £40, but these should be confirmed with the club directly since published guides can lag behind current pricing.

The par-4 10th, at 435 yards, has a reputation as one of the harder par-4s in Irish links golf. It's the kind of hole that you're still thinking about over dinner.

## Building the Circuit

A practical County Down golf trip might look like two or three nights in Newcastle, using the Slieve Donard Resort (directly adjacent to Royal County Down) or the Burrendale Hotel nearby as a base.

From Newcastle, Ardglass is a 30-minute drive and Kirkistown is around an hour. Belfast's two airports, George Best Belfast City and Belfast International, are both usable, with Newcastle roughly 45 minutes from the city via the A24. If you're coming from Scotland, the Cairnryan ferry is a viable alternative to flying.

One thing worth noting for anyone coming from the Republic of Ireland or mixing itineraries: County Down uses GBP, not euros. It's Northern Ireland, not the Republic.

Kilkeel Golf Club, about 12 miles from Royal County Down, served as a qualifying venue for the British Amateur and the Senior British Open, and is worth adding if you're extending the stay to four or five days. Warrenpoint, an established parkland course a little further around the bay, rounds out a longer itinerary for those who want contrast from pure links golf.

## The Honest Takeaway

The case for a County Down golf circuit rather than a Royal County Down pilgrimage is straightforward. The logistics of getting to Newcastle aren't trivial from most parts of the UK mainland, and the Championship Links, for all its excellence, has green fee and booking realities that mean two rounds there in one trip isn't a given. Ardglass and Kirkistown are genuinely good courses, not consolation prizes, and they're accessible in a way that Royal County Down simply isn't. Plan the Championship Links as your centrepiece, build the other two around it, and the trip justifies itself on every level.
