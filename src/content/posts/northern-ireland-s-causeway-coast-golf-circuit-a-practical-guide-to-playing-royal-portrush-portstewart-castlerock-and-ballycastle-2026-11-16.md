## More Than One Course on This Coastline

Royal Portrush gets most of the attention, and fairly enough. It is the only club in Ireland to have hosted The Open Championship, most recently in 2019, and the Dunluce Links is as good as links golf gets anywhere in these islands. But flying in for a single round and flying home again means missing what makes this stretch of the North Antrim coast genuinely special: a cluster of championship-quality links courses sitting within a short drive of each other, with the Atlantic doing its thing in the background throughout.

A three or four-day Causeway Coast golf circuit, built around Portrush as the centrepiece but taking in Portstewart, Castlerock and Ballycastle as well, is one of the more rewarding domestic golf trips available to UK golfers. This guide covers the practicalities of getting there, where to base yourself, and how to sequence the courses sensibly.

---

## Getting There From Mainland Britain

Northern Ireland is a domestic trip, which means no passport faff and no currency exchange. The most straightforward option from Scotland is the ferry from Cairnryan, with both Stena Line (to Belfast) and P&O Ferries (to Larne) operating frequent daily crossings of around two hours. From Larne you can drive straight up the Antrim coast road, which is a reasonable journey in itself. The Belfast sailings from Birkenhead in Liverpool take around eight hours and suit an overnight crossing if you want to wake up ready to play.

Once you are there, hire a car. There is a train from Belfast Grand Central to Coleraine (roughly an hour and twenty minutes, running roughly hourly) and onward to Portrush, and the journey is reportedly scenic enough, tracking close to the Mussenden Temple on the clifftop at one point. But moving between four golf clubs across a few days without a car will make the logistics awkward. Book one.

## Where to Stay

Portrush and Portstewart are both practical bases and sit only a few miles apart. Either works well: you are a short drive from Royal Portrush and Portstewart Golf Club, around fifteen to twenty minutes from Castlerock to the west, and roughly twenty minutes from Ballycastle to the east. The towns are compact and well set up for visitors, with accommodation ranging from guesthouses and B&Bs to larger hotels. Book early for summer visits, particularly around any major golf events in the area.

## The Courses: What to Expect

### Royal Portrush

The Dunluce Links (par 72) is the main event and one of the top ten courses in the British Isles by most assessments. The Valley Links (par 71) is a worthwhile course in its own right and considerably more accessible to book independently.

Visitor access to the Dunluce Links is restricted, and during peak season the most reliable route in is through a golf travel company. If you are planning well in advance, that is the approach to take. Do not assume you can book a tee time online six weeks out and secure a slot on a busy summer Saturday.

### Portstewart Golf Club

The Strand Course (par 72) opens with a stretch through towering dunes that is frequently cited as one of the great front nines in Ireland. The golf is exposed and demanding without being unfair. Portstewart also has the shorter Riverside Course (par 68) and Old Course (par 64), which are useful if someone in your group wants a less punishing day or if you have a free morning to fill. Visitor access here is more straightforward than at Portrush, though advance booking for peak weeks is still sensible.

### Castlerock Golf Club

Founded in 1901, Castlerock sits where the River Bann meets the sea and the Mussenden Course (par 73) has the credentials to match: it appears in UK and Ireland top 100 rankings and plays every bit like proper links golf should, with the dunes, the wind, and the full set of excuses if your round goes sideways. The nine-hole Bann Course offers an alternative if time or energy is short. Castlerock is a short drive west of Portstewart and fits neatly into a circuit.

### Ballycastle Golf Club

Around twenty minutes east of Portrush, Ballycastle (founded 1890, par 71) is a hybrid course: part links, part parkland, rising to higher ground with views across to Rathlin Island, Fair Head and the Mull of Kintyre on a clear day. At just under 5,900 yards from the main tees it is not the most demanding course on this list, but the setting is exceptional and it makes a good opener or closer to a trip, particularly for mixed-ability groups. It is semi-private and open to visitors.

A mention also for Bushfoot Golf Club near Bushmills, a nine-hole course that works well as a half-day warm-up or a relaxed evening round without taking a full day's slot.

## Sequencing the Circuit

A sensible order for four days might look like this: start with Ballycastle or Castlerock to get your links legs back (both more accessible and slightly less pressurised), move to the Portstewart Strand Course on day two, play Royal Portrush Dunluce Links on day three when you are sharp and the booking you made months ago finally arrives, and use day four for the Valley Links at Portrush or a second round at whichever course you felt you left unfinished.

Three days works too if you drop one of the smaller venues. The key is not to treat this as a sprint to Portrush and back.

## The Broader Context

This coastline has produced three Major champions: Fred Daly, Darren Clarke, and Graeme McDowell all grew up here. That is not coincidence. The conditions that produced those players, the wind off the Atlantic, the firm and fast fairways, the greens that reward imagination rather than just power, are still there every day for visiting golfers willing to put in the planning.

The Causeway Coast golf circuit is a proper trip, not a one-course pilgrimage. Four days, four courses, and a ferry crossing from the Scottish mainland. That is a very good few days of golf.
