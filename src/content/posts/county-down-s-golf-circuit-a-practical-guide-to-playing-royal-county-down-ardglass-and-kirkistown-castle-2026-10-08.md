County Down is one of those corners of the golfing world that gets reduced to a single destination in most people's minds. Royal County Down sits at the top of almost every world ranking, so the trip becomes: get the tee time, fly in, play it, fly home. Which is understandable, but it also means you're missing two courses that genuinely deserve your time, and skipping a logistical approach that makes the whole trip significantly easier to put together.

This guide covers County Down's golf circuit as a three-course trip: Royal County Down, Ardglass, and Kirkistown Castle. How to actually get on each one, what they're like to play, and how to string the days together.

---

## The Three Courses

### Royal County Down, Newcastle

RCD needs little introduction. The Championship Links, founded in 1889 and stretching to 7,186 yards from the tips, sits wedged between the Mourne Mountains and the Irish Sea at Newcastle. It is, by almost any measure, one of the most visually arresting courses on the planet.

Playing it is another matter. Blind tee shots are a recurring feature, and several of them are genuinely demanding rather than quirky for the sake of it. The 9th is the most famous: your tee shot goes over a dune with the fairway shifting below, and if you haven't played the hole before you're largely trusting your caddie. Which is exactly why caddies aren't just recommended here, they're close to essential. Forecaddies are sometimes mandatory for groups of three or four, and you should budget accordingly.

There's also an Annesley Links on site, which is a useful option if Championship dates prove elusive.

**Getting a tee time:** This is where most people come unstuck. Visitor access on the Championship Links is restricted to specific days: Monday, Tuesday and Friday (mornings and afternoons), Thursday mornings, and Sunday afternoons. The tee sheet typically opens around 1 April for the following season. When it does, it fills fast. You need to call the club directly; there's no online request system. Full payment is required at the time of booking.

The advice here is simple: call as close to opening day as possible, be flexible on dates, and don't assume you'll get your preferred round on your preferred day. Contact the club directly for current availability and pricing, as both change year to year.

### Ardglass Golf Club

About 30 minutes from Newcastle along the coast, Ardglass is the circuit's hidden gem, or at least the one that gets talked about far less than it deserves.

Established in 1896, the course sits on clifftops above the Irish Sea, which comes into play on eight holes and is visible from all 18. The opening five holes hug the cliff edge. It's a par 70 measuring around 6,268 yards, which sounds modest until the wind picks up off the water.

The clubhouse is worth a mention in its own right: part of it occupies a fortified building dating to the 15th century, which is a credible claim to being one of the oldest clubhouse structures in world golf.

Visitors are generally welcome, with some restrictions at weekends (pre-booking required), and no handicap certificate is needed. Green fees are substantially lower than RCD, and the course is widely regarded as excellent value. Compared to the Championship Links, it's a very different proposition financially while still offering a genuine links test.

### Kirkistown Castle Golf Club, Cloughey

On the Ards Peninsula, roughly 45 minutes from Belfast, Kirkistown Castle is the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland and, as the nearest links to Belfast, often the easiest to access.

The layout was revised by James Braid in 1934. At around 6,167 yards off the medal tees and par 70, it won't intimidate on paper, but elevated tees and greens and a layout that demands precision make it more testing than the yardage suggests. The par-4 10th has a reputation as one of the tougher par 4s in Irish golf.

One practical advantage stands out: the course is built on a free-draining sandy base, which means it's rarely closed even after significant rain. If you're travelling in autumn or winter, that matters. The course has been recognised as the best value golf experience in Ulster, and it's hard to argue. On site there's a pro shop, practice facilities, and Charlie's Bar and Bistro for afterwards.

Visitors are welcome. It's the most straightforward of the three to book onto and a good start to any trip.

## Getting There and Getting Around

From mainland UK, your two airport options are Belfast City (BHD) and Belfast International (BFS). BHD is closer to the County Down coast, around 30 to 40 minutes from Newcastle by car. BFS adds roughly another 20 minutes. Both airports receive flights from London, Manchester, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Liverpool, Bristol, and elsewhere.

If you're bringing your own clubs and have the time, the ferry is worth considering. P&O Irish Sea and Stena Line both run crossings from Cairnryan or Stranraer to Larne or Belfast, which puts you in reasonable range of all three courses once you've driven off.

A car is not optional for this trip. The three clubs are spread across the County Down and Ards coastline, and public transport doesn't connect them usefully.

## A Logical Three-Day Order

Start at Kirkistown. It's the easiest to book, welcomes visitors without fuss, and plays well in any conditions. A good day to find your feet after travelling.

Day two at Ardglass gives you the clifftop links, a different kind of drama, and a genuine test that won't feel like a warm-up act.

Save Royal County Down for the final day. The bucket-list course works better as a closer than an opener, partly because the pressure of the trip's centrepiece is easier to carry when you've already had two good days on the course.

For accommodation, the Slieve Donard Resort and Spa sits directly adjacent to RCD in Newcastle and is the obvious base if proximity is the priority. The Burrendale Hotel in Newcastle offers a less expensive alternative. Staying in Belfast opens up more choices on price and variety, at the cost of adding 50-plus minutes of driving each morning.

---

## The Actual Takeaway

County Down's golf circuit works because the three courses are genuinely complementary. Kirkistown gives you access and value; Ardglass gives you drama and a bit of history; Royal County Down gives you the course most golfers will only play once and will talk about for years. Book RCD first, build the other two days around it, and hire a caddie for the Championship Links. The rest tends to fall into place.
