Southwest Ireland's golf circuit is one of those trips that gets talked about in clubhouses for years, usually by people who either did it properly and loved it, or squeezed it into four days and spent the whole journey home calculating what they'd missed. This guide is aimed at helping you do the former.

The focus here is Kerry and Clare, where more than 50 courses are packed into a stretch of Atlantic coastline that punches well above its weight. This is not a bucket-list rundown. It is a practical map of how to route the trip, which courses actually deserve your limited tee times, and where the planning tends to go wrong.

## Flying In: Start at Shannon, Not Cork

Shannon Airport is the sensible entry point for this circuit. Lahinch is around 45 minutes away, Ballybunion roughly 90 minutes, and Waterville under three hours once you pick up the Ring of Kerry. Kerry Airport, near Killarney, works well if you want to reverse the loop or anchor the trip in the south first. Cork is an option but adds driving time to the courses that matter most here.

A car is non-negotiable. Rural Kerry and Clare have limited public transport and several of the courses you will want to play are well off the main roads. Sort the hire car before you land.

## How Long You Actually Need

Four days is possible but you will finish the trip regretting it. Five to seven days gives you breathing room and, crucially, a buffer day for the Atlantic weather doing what the Atlantic weather does. Links golf in Ireland requires some tolerance for wind and the odd grey morning. Building in a spare slot means a washed-out afternoon at one course does not collapse the rest of the schedule.

## The Courses Worth Prioritising

**Lahinch Old Course (Co. Clare)** is the natural first stop off Shannon. Founded in 1892, originally laid out by Old Tom Morris and later redesigned by Alister MacKenzie, it is a proper championship links with blind shots, fast-running fairways, and the kind of quirks that make links golf divisive and brilliant in equal measure. Book this one early, because it books early.

**Ballybunion Old Course (Co. Kerry)** is frequently cited as one of the finest links courses in the world, and on the right day in the right conditions it earns that reputation. Founded in 1893, with holes that sit dramatically on clifftops above the Shannon Estuary. The club can show as sold out when you search directly; specialist golf travel operators sometimes have availability when the club's own calendar looks full. Worth checking both routes when you book.

**Waterville (Co. Kerry)** sits at the far end of the Ring of Kerry loop and its remoteness is part of the appeal. It is a long drive from most bases but the course rewards the effort, sitting between mountain and ocean. If you are doing the Ring of Kerry anyway, it fits naturally into the routing.

**Tralee (Co. Kerry)**, designed by Arnold Palmer and described as his first European course design, offers views across Tralee Bay and a layout that is visually spectacular if uneven in its demands. Worth including if your schedule allows.

**Dooks (Co. Kerry)** is lower-profile than the headline names but golf has been played there since 1889. It sits on Dingle Bay and tends to be quieter and more affordable, which makes it a useful balancing act in a trip otherwise heavy on prestige venues.

**Doonbeg (Co. Clare)** is a Greg Norman design on the Clare coast with 16 holes carrying Atlantic views. Worth knowing: the Amgen Irish Open is scheduled there for September 2026 (10-13 September), which will affect tee-time availability in that window.

**Old Head Golf Links (near Kinsale, Co. Cork)** sits on a headland peninsula jutting into the Atlantic. It is a detour south from the main Kerry-Clare corridor, so factor in the driving time if you include it.

**Adare Manor (Co. Limerick)** is a parkland course, not a links, but it makes a logical anchor for the start or end of the trip. The Tom Fazio redesign reopened in 2017 and the venue is confirmed as host of the 2027 Ryder Cup.

## Booking: Do Not Leave It

The top clubs book out months ahead, sometimes for visitor tee times in the prime summer and early autumn window. Ballybunion and Lahinch in particular. Booking direct is straightforward but the availability genuinely disappears. If you are travelling in September 2026 specifically, note that the Walker Cup takes place at Lahinch on 5-6 September. Tee-time availability at both Lahinch and Doonbeg will be tighter around that fortnight than usual.

September is generally considered one of the more pleasant months for links golf in Ireland. Visitor tee times at some clubs reduce from mid-October, so early autumn is broadly the sweet spot before access starts to narrow.

## A Few Practical Points

Most championship links here do not allow buggies, or restrict their use to medical necessity. If that affects you, confirm the policy when you book rather than on arrival. Caddies are recommended at the flagship venues, both for course knowledge and because carrying a bag over 6,000-plus yards of Atlantic links is a different proposition from your home parkland.

The Wild Atlantic Way driving route connects many of these courses naturally, and following it loosely gives the trip a logical shape without requiring rigid planning.

## The Honest Takeaway

Southwest Ireland's golf circuit rewards the golfer who plans at least six months out, builds in more days than they think they need, and accepts that some of the best moments will come from courses not on anyone's top-ten list. Book the marquee tee times first, build the rest of the trip around them, and do not underestimate the driving distances once you are on Kerry's rural roads.
