There are stretches of English coastline that golfers talk about constantly, and stretches that quietly get on with it. The Yorkshire east coast falls firmly into the second camp. Within roughly thirty miles of road you can play a James Braid clifftop design, a genuine headland links perched above chalk cliffs, an inland course with more major pedigree than most golfers realise, and a couple of solid clubs that make excellent fillers on a multi-day trip. Build a two- or three-night stay around Bridlington, Flamborough, Filey and Scarborough and you've got a circuit that holds its own against most of England's celebrated coastal itineraries, without the crowds or the waiting lists.

This is a practical guide to doing exactly that.

---

## What to Expect from This Stretch of Coast

The east Yorkshire coastline isn't links golf in the Lincolnshire or Lancashire sense. What you get instead is variety: clifftop courses where the wind genuinely matters, hybrid layouts that mix open turf with tree-lined sections, and one inland outlier that plays more like a links than most courses sitting next to the sea. The coastal wind is the consistent factor throughout. Club selection and shot shape matter here in a way they simply don't on sheltered parkland tracks, so come prepared to think.

The area is accessible. The A64 corridor puts it roughly 1.5 to 2 hours from York, Leeds and most of the North of England. There's rail to Scarborough and Bridlington, though getting to Flamborough or Filey from the train requires a car or taxi. A long weekend works well, with a base in Scarborough or Bridlington covering most of the courses on the circuit.

## The Courses

### Bridlington Golf Club (Belvedere)

Belvedere opened in 1905 and carries a James Braid design credit, which tells you something about the age and character of the place. It sits between the Yorkshire Wolds and the North Sea, par 72, stretching to around 6,700 yards from the white tees. Visitors are welcome, and the club has a semi-private feel rather than the slightly formal atmosphere that some older clubs carry. It's a solid, honest course rather than a showstopper, and that's not a criticism. It earns its place on a multi-round trip.

### Bridlington Links Golf and Leisure Estate

A separate venue from Belvedere and a noticeably different proposition. The Links sits on clifftop ground at Danes Dyke with views across Bridlington Bay, and at around 6,700 yards par 72 it has a more exposed, links-influenced feel. The clifftop holes are the obvious draw. Worth playing on the same trip as Belvedere rather than choosing between them.

**Practical note:** The Golf Coast Passport covers both Bridlington clubs alongside Flamborough Head, Filey and Hornsea, and represents good value for visiting groups playing multiple rounds. Check current availability and pricing directly with the participating clubs.

### Flamborough Head Golf Club

This is the one that tends to surprise people. Founded in 1931, Flamborough Head is an 18-hole clifftop course moulded around the contours of the headland itself, par 71 at roughly 6,200 yards. The setting is dramatic in a way that's hard to oversell: the Old Beacon beside the 5th tee is reputedly the only chalk lighthouse of its kind in England, built around 1674. The course plays with and against the headland's natural shape, and the wind off the North Sea can make even straightforward holes feel like problem-solving exercises.

Visitors are welcome every day, though advance booking is strongly recommended. The club describes itself as the friendliest on the coast, and by most accounts it backs that up. Green fees should be confirmed directly with the club, as current pricing wasn't confirmed in research for this piece.

### Filey Golf Club

Established in 1897, Filey blends links and parkland characteristics in a way that suits the landscape well. The panoramic sea views are a genuine feature rather than a background detail, and the club welcomes visitors with equipment hire available for those travelling without a full set. It doesn't have the dramatic clifftop drama of Flamborough, but it's a well-maintained and enjoyable course that fits naturally into a circuit round the bay. Confirm current visitor fees and tee time availability before travelling.

### Scarborough South Cliff Golf Club

South Cliff offers a mix of parkland and clifftop holes with solid facilities, including a clubhouse and pro shop. It's a reliable option for rounding out a trip, particularly if you're based in Scarborough. It's not the most talked-about course on this stretch, but it's a decent track in a good location and visitors are well catered for.

## The Detour Worth Making: Ganton Golf Club

Ganton sits 15 to 20 minutes inland from Scarborough and belongs in a different conversation from the coastal clubs, but it would be wrong not to mention it. Founded in 1891, it has hosted the Ryder Cup (1949), the Curtis Cup (2000) and the Walker Cup (2003), and it remains the only inland course to have hosted the Amateur Championship, which it's done three times. The design has been worked on over the years by Harry Vardon, James Braid, J.H. Taylor, Harry Colt, Alister MacKenzie and Tom Simpson. The sandy subsoil gives the course a genuine links character despite being well away from the sea: tight bents-and-fescue fairways, deep bunkers and dense gorse that punishes anything wayward.

A current WHS handicap index is required to play. Visitors can book through the club office, and Dormy House accommodation is available on-site, which makes a stay-and-play arrangement straightforward. Treat any green fee figures you find online as indicative at best, given how quickly visitor pricing moves at courses of this calibre, and check directly with the club for current rates.

## Putting a Trip Together

Two nights gives you four or five rounds comfortably, depending on how early you're willing to start. Three nights lets you add Ganton without turning the trip into a slog. Bridlington or Scarborough both work as a base, with most of the courses reachable inside 30 to 40 minutes from either. The Golf Coast Passport is worth investigating for groups wanting to keep costs organised across the Bridlington and Filey clubs.

The honest takeaway from this stretch is that it rewards a bit of planning but doesn't demand it. These are clubs that are used to visiting golfers, the booking processes are generally straightforward, and the courses are varied enough that five rounds won't feel repetitive. Yorkshire's east coast golf circuit is a compact, purposeful trip. It just hasn't been marketed as loudly as it deserves.
