Scotland tends to get the bucket-list traffic. Ireland gets the romanticists. Wales, somehow, keeps quietly producing links golf that sits comfortably in the Top 100 GB&I rankings while most visiting golfers drive straight past on their way somewhere else. That is, to put it plainly, a situation worth exploiting.

Wales has three courses in the top 100 of the GB&I rankings for 2025/26 and nine more in the next hundred. The top three — Royal Porthcawl, Royal St David's at Harlech, and Pennard — are all driveable within a single self-drive trip, spread across South Wales and the Snowdonia coast. Different in character, all legitimately excellent, and collectively a lot less hectic than a week in Fife.

Here is how to approach each one, and how to string the three together sensibly.

---

## Royal Porthcawl: Wales's Top-Ranked Links

Ranked 33rd in GB&I and the clear No.1 in Wales, Porthcawl sits just off the M4 and makes an immediate impression. Sea views are present from every single hole, which is rarer than the claim suggests on most coastal courses. The routing splits into an outer front nine following the shoreline and a more enclosed back nine, giving the round a natural shift in feel at the turn.

The design history reflects the course's ambition: original work from Ramsay Hunter in 1898, H.S. Colt revising it in 1919, and Martin Ebert's more recent adjustments in 2011. The result is a course that feels genuinely settled in its land rather than imposed on it.

The 18th, a 459-yard par 4 playing downhill toward the ocean, is a proper finishing hole. The course has hosted the Senior Open twice in recent years, with Alex Cejka winning the 2023 edition, and that pedigree shows in the challenge it presents even when conditions are benign.

Visitor tee times are available with practice facilities and a traditional clubhouse on site. Expect to pay a premium green fee by Welsh standards, though it remains competitive against equivalent courses in Scotland or the Republic.

---

## Royal St David's, Harlech: Tough Par, Spectacular Setting

About 2.5 to 3 hours north of Porthcawl, Royal St David's asks you to work for every shot. Established in 1894 and ranked 57th in GB&I, the course measures just over 6,600 yards from the championship tees and has a reputation for one of the toughest scratch pars in the Top 100 UK&I. Distance alone does not explain the difficulty: the pot bunkers are punishing, the back nine pushes deep into the dunes from the 14th, and the course finishes, unusually, with a par 3 at the 18th.

The setting compounds the psychological challenge. The course runs beneath Harlech Castle, a UNESCO World Heritage-listed fortress, with Snowdonia filling the background. It is genuinely arresting, which is not always helpful when you are trying to work out the wind direction.

The 14th, a long blind par 3, and the unconventional finish are the holes that tend to stick in the memory. Royal St David's is also noted by National Club Golfer as among the more affordable courses in the Top 100, making it one of the more practical ways to play a genuinely world-class links.

A dormy house is available for stay-and-play arrangements, which makes an overnight here very easy to justify.

---

## Pennard: Links in the Sky, Gower Peninsula

Pennard is the most distinctive of the three and probably the most underrated in wider conversation. Situated on the Gower Peninsula (the UK's first Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty), about 8 miles west of Swansea, the course sits 200 feet above sea level. The nickname "the links in the sky" is not an exaggeration.

Founded in 1896 and shaped over time by James Braid and C.K. Cotton, Pennard plays to 6,225 yards at par 71. The exposure means wind management is not a minor consideration but the entire strategic challenge. Recent visitors have noted some blind shots and limited yardage markers in places, which rewards local knowledge on a second visit.

The 7th, known as Castle, plays between the ruins of a 13th-century church and Pennard Castle itself. It is one of the more atmospheric holes in Welsh golf. Cattle occasionally graze on the common land portions of the course, which either charms you or catches you off-guard depending on temperament.

Ranked 70th in GB&I and No.3 in Wales, Pennard punches well above what its relative anonymity might suggest.

---

## The Itinerary: How to Combine All Three

Porthcawl and Pennard are both in South Wales and within roughly an hour of each other, which makes them a natural pairing. Two nights based in or around Swansea or Bridgend covers both, with time for dinner and a proper look at the Gower if you want it.

From there, the drive north to Harlech takes two and a half to three hours depending on your route. It is not a short hop, but it is entirely manageable and the scenery through the Welsh mountains is worth the driving time in its own right. Finish the trip with a night near Harlech and a day on the course, then head home.

Three rounds, three very different characters, and a total green fee bill that compares well with a single round at some of the more famous Scottish venues.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

Wales does not need to be positioned as a consolation prize for golfers who could not get to Scotland. Porthcawl, Harlech, and Pennard stand on their own merits in any serious Top 100 conversation. The practical advantages (lower visitor volumes, competitive costs, a self-drive route that actually holds together) are useful, but the golf itself is the reason to go. If you have been meaning to tick these off and keep postponing, this is the straightforward argument for just booking it.
