Most UK golfers who make the trip to County Down have one thing in mind: Royal County Down. That's understandable. It's been ranked the world's number one course by Golf Digest for 2024, it sits below the Mourne Mountains with the Irish Sea at its shoulder, and it's the kind of place that stays with you. But if you fly in, play Newcastle, and fly home, you've seen roughly a third of what County Down's golf coast actually offers.

The county has the makings of a proper three-round trip, with Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle completing a circuit that covers the coastline from the Ards Peninsula down to the Mournes. This guide covers the practical side of making that trip work: visitor access, what to expect at each club, and how to piece the three days together sensibly.

## Royal County Down: Brilliant, But Plan Carefully

The Championship Course at Newcastle is par 71, stretching to 7,186 yards, and the founding name on the design is Old Tom Morris, with later input from Harry Colt. All of that is well documented. What catches some visitors out is the restricted access.

Visitor tee times on the Championship Course are available on Mondays, Tuesdays and Thursday mornings, Friday and Sunday afternoons only. That's it. There are no exceptions for walking up on a Wednesday and hoping for the best. Advance booking is essential, and given the demand the course attracts, booking well in advance is the sensible approach.

Peak-season green fees for 2026 are £450, up from £425 in 2025. That's a significant sum, and it's risen sharply over the past couple of years. Societies and golf packages can offer an alternative route to securing times if you're coming as a group.

If your budget or schedule doesn't stretch to the Championship Course, the Annesley Links on the same site is a par 66 layout at 4,548 yards. It's shorter and considerably more accessible, and it still gives you the Newcastle setting and the mountain backdrop.

## Ardglass: Clifftop Links With a Genuinely Remarkable Clubhouse

Ardglass Golf Club sits around 30 miles south of Belfast, and it deserves far more attention than it typically gets outside golfing circles. The course is par 70, just over 6,200 yards, and the front five holes run along the Irish Sea coastline at close enough range that you're aware of the water on almost every shot.

The clubhouse is based in Ardglass Castle, parts of which date to 1405. The claim that it's the oldest clubhouse in the world is widely repeated, and it's one of those details that makes the place feel genuinely different from your standard club visit.

Visitors are welcome every day at Ardglass, which makes scheduling considerably more flexible than at Royal County Down. A handicap certificate is required. The club's practice facilities are limited by most accounts, so if you're looking to loosen up beforehand, manage expectations there. Peak-season visitor green fees are around £220, based on 2025 figures.

On a clear day, you can see the Isle of Man from parts of the course. There's a halfway cottage at the back of the 7th green. Small details, but they add up to a place that rewards a slower pace.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Ards Peninsula's Understated Links

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club sits on the eastern tip of the Ards Peninsula near Cloughey, which makes it the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland. Founded in 1902, the layout was updated by James Braid in 1934. It plays as a par 70, in the region of 6,200 yards depending on the tees used.

It's also the closest links course to Belfast, around 45 minutes by road, which gives it a practical edge for golfers building a County Down itinerary from the city. Visitors are welcome every day, though advance booking is advised. On GolfPass the club rates at 4.6 out of 5, with 93% of reviewers recommending it, largely on the grounds of friendliness and atmosphere.

Green fees are meaningfully lower than the other two courses on this circuit. Rather than state a specific figure that may not be current, the honest summary is that Kirkistown Castle is positioned as the best-value links in Ulster, and the reviews consistently reflect that assessment.

## Building the Three-Day Circuit

The geography works in your favour. Kirkistown Castle is on the north-east tip of County Down. Ardglass sits on the south-east coast. Royal County Down anchors the south-west corner at Newcastle. You can move between the three in a logical loop, or use Newcastle as a base and drive out to the others from there.

Newcastle is around 45 minutes from Belfast city via the A24. The Slieve Donard Resort sits directly adjacent to Royal County Down and is the standard recommendation for golfers wanting to walk to the first tee. There are other hotels in Newcastle at lower price points if the resort's rates don't suit.

For getting there from mainland UK, both Belfast City Airport and Belfast International serve the journey well. City Airport is the closer option to County Down destinations, typically 30 to 40 minutes by car. If you'd rather not put your clubs in an airline hold, the car ferry from Cairnryan in Scotland (Stena Line and P&O Irish Sea) or from Liverpool (Stena Line) is a practical alternative that a number of UK golfers use specifically for this reason.

## The Short Version

Royal County Down justifies every superlative it receives, but the trip becomes genuinely exceptional when you add Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle around it. The restricted visitor windows at Newcastle require you to plan the schedule in a specific order anyway, and once you've done that planning, fitting three rounds into three days is straightforward. Don't arrive expecting the same open-access culture you get at most UK links clubs. Do arrive expecting the courses to be worth it.
