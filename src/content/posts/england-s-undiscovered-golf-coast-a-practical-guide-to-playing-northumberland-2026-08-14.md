## The Coast That Gets Overlooked

Mention a seaside golf trip in the UK and most golfers immediately think Scotland or Ireland. East Lothian, Fife, Doonbeg, Royal County Down. The usual suspects. Northumberland barely gets a look-in, which is genuinely baffling when you consider what's there: seven links and downland courses clustered along roughly 20 miles of coastline, sitting in one of England's most striking Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty, with Bamburgh Castle on the headland and Dunstanburgh's ruined towers looming over the fairways.

A practical guide to playing Northumberland really ought to begin with this: you will almost certainly have the courses to yourself compared with anywhere on the Scottish side of the border. The region gets a fraction of the golf tourism its quality deserves, which means better tee-time availability, less pressure, and a more relaxed experience overall.

## The Courses Worth Building a Trip Around

### Bamburgh Castle Golf Club

Established in 1904, Bamburgh Castle is the centrepiece for most visiting golfers. It's a clifftop links at par 68 and just over 5,600 yards, which sounds short on paper until gorse, heather, basalt rock outcrops, and a coastal wind get involved. The views from the course are genuinely spectacular: Bamburgh Castle itself, the Farne Islands out to sea, and Holy Island (Lindisfarne) visible on a clear day. It's a course where the setting does a lot of the work, but there's proper golf to be played too.

A few things to know before you book. The club is cashless, so bring a card. Saturday tee times are reserved for member competitions, and slots before 10am during the week are also blocked for members. Book midweek, plan for late morning, and confirm timings directly with the club. Green fees vary by season, so check the current pricing on their website before you go.

### Dunstanburgh Castle Golf Club

A couple of miles further down the coast, Dunstanburgh wraps around Embleton Bay with the ruins of a 14th-century castle as its backdrop. The course was designed by James Braid and founded in 1900. It's open to the public and visitor-friendly, with a clubhouse that does food and drink. The whole site sits within the Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty designation, and the combination of bay, dunes, and medieval ruin is hard to beat for atmosphere. This one punches well above its profile.

### Alnmouth Golf Club

Alnmouth has a strong claim to being the fourth oldest golf club in England, dating from 1869. The course was redesigned by H.S. Colt in 1930 and plays to par 71 at just over 6,500 yards from the men's tees. It's coastal parkland rather than pure links turf, which gives it a different character from Bamburgh and Dunstanburgh. The big practical detail here is the dormy house: 26 beds on site, which makes a stay-and-play arrangement straightforward and avoids messing around with external accommodation logistics.

Visitors are welcome every day except Saturday. A handicap certificate is required, and advance booking is essential. Guide prices have been quoted in the region of £60 per round midweek, but treat that as approximate. Confirm current fees directly with the club before planning your budget around it.

### The Others Worth Knowing About

Alnmouth Village Golf Club is a separate entity from Alnmouth GC (a point of genuine local confusion). It's a 9-hole/18-tee layout designed by Mungo Park in 1869, playing to par 71 at around 6,090 yards. Visiting golfers are welcome.

Goswick Links is a James Braid design that the R&A recognises as an Open qualifying course. That alone tells you something about its quality and condition. It sits at the southern end of the coastal cluster and rounds out a natural three-round itinerary nicely.

Seahouses Golf Club offers a shorter, more relaxed option if your group has mixed appetites for serious golf versus the scenery and the local seafood.

## How to Sequence a Trip

A three-round trip over two to three nights is the natural format. Bamburgh, Dunstanburgh, and Goswick sit close enough together to do one per day without excessive driving. Alnmouth GC makes sense as a fourth round if you want to extend the trip or use the dormy house as a base.

There are organised packages available combining two or three of these courses with local accommodation, available through the clubs directly and through golf travel operators. Worth searching for rather than booking each element separately, particularly outside peak summer. Any packages mentioned here are informational only, check directly with the clubs for current availability and pricing.

One practical sequencing note: avoid anchoring any of your rounds on a Saturday. Both Bamburgh and Alnmouth GC restrict visitor access on Saturdays. Plan around Thursday to Sunday travel, with your golf on Thursday, Friday, and Sunday if you want a weekend departure.

## Getting There and Around

Northumberland sits between Newcastle and the Scottish border, less than an hour from Newcastle via the A1. That makes it easy to reach by train to Newcastle and then hire car. It also positions it well as part of a longer road trip that takes in East Lothian's courses over the border, a natural pairing if you have the time (covered separately on this site).

## The Case for Going

Northumberland's golf coast doesn't have the marketing budget of its Scottish neighbours, and it probably never will. What it does have is proper links golf, dramatic coastal scenery, visitor-friendly clubs that aren't overwhelmed with tour groups, and a slower pace than anywhere south of it. The combination of Braid designs, a genuine slice of English golf history at Alnmouth, and the sheer visual drama of playing beneath Bamburgh Castle makes this one of the most compelling golf trips in England. It just needs more people to notice.

Book midweek, avoid Saturdays, confirm fees directly with each club, and give yourself at least two full days on the coast.
