## The Case for Doing County Down Properly

Most visiting golfers come for Royal County Down and leave. That is entirely understandable. It is, by most credible rankings, one of the finest golf courses on the planet. But if you are flying into Belfast, hiring a car, and spending money on a green fee that will make your eyes water, it seems a shame to treat the surrounding coastline as mere scenery.

County Down's golf coast offers something genuinely rare: a world-ranked flagship, a clifftop gem that most English visitors have never heard of, and a no-frills traditional links that rounds out a proper three-day circuit. This guide covers all three, with practical notes on access, logistics, and what to expect.

---

## Day One: Royal County Down, Newcastle

There is not much left to say about Royal County Down that has not already been said, but the basics are worth stating clearly. The Championship Links at Newcastle was founded in 1889, shaped by Old Tom Morris and later refined by Harry Vardon and Harry Colt, and sits against the backdrop of the Mourne Mountains rolling down to the dunes. Golf Digest ranked it the world's number one course in its 2024 list. It has hosted the Irish Open, most recently the 2024 Amgen Irish Open, and it is the kind of place that tends to stay with you.

**What you need to know before you book**

Visitor access is restricted to designated days only, broadly reported as Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday mornings, plus Friday and Sunday afternoons. This can and does change, so check the club's own site at royalcountydown.org before planning around it. Visitor season runs broadly from April through October.

The green fee sits firmly in the premium bracket. A third-party travel source has cited a figure of around £450 for 2026, but this has not been confirmed by the club directly, and pricing at this level tends to change. Treat any figure you read online as indicative and verify with the club when booking.

A few practical points worth taking seriously: there are no buggies. The course has a number of blind tee shots, and a caddie is not just a nice-to-have here but a genuine advantage, particularly on a first visit. The second course, the Annesley Links, offers a less demanding but still enjoyable alternative if you are making a day of it.

Newcastle itself is a pleasant base, roughly 50 minutes south of Belfast via the A24.

## Day Two: Ardglass Golf Club

Ardglass sits about 35 minutes along the coast from Newcastle and deserves far more attention than it typically gets from visiting golfers. This is a clifftop links in the proper sense, carved into the coastline above Ardglass village, with views across the Irish Sea that make it difficult to concentrate on your score.

The opening hole is the talking point, a par four that drops dramatically from an elevated tee at the cliff edge. It sets the tone for a course that is relentlessly scenic and genuinely good golf. At par 70, it is not the longest test in the world, but it plays to its setting and its natural terrain rather than against them.

The clubhouse is reportedly housed in a 14th-century castle building, which makes it one of the oldest structures anywhere used as a golf clubhouse. Whether or not that claim stands up to rigorous historical scrutiny, the building itself is worth a look.

Ardglass is visitor-friendly and considerably more affordable than Royal County Down. No confirmed green fee from the club is available at the time of writing, so contact the club directly for current pricing. It has been ranked among the top ten courses in Northern Ireland, and by some measures higher, which feels about right.

---

## Day Three: Kirkistown Castle Golf Club

Kirkistown Castle, on the Ards Peninsula near Cloughey, is the kind of course that gets quietly championed by people who have played it and overlooked by everyone else. Designed by James Braid in 1902, it is described as the most easterly golf course in Ireland, sitting on fast-and-firm links turf on a peninsula that gets plenty of wind off the sea.

At 6,167 yards off the main tees and par 69, it is not a monster. But links golf at this kind of course is rarely about length. The turf, the wind, and the bounce are the variables that matter, and Kirkistown has all three in quantity. The atmosphere is relaxed and welcoming, the kind of members' club that does not make visitors feel like an inconvenience.

Guideline green fees from third-party sources suggest midweek play from around £35 and weekends from around £40, though these figures should be verified with the club directly as they may not be current.

Kirkistown is roughly an hour from Newcastle, and also accessible from Belfast, which makes it a reasonable final leg before heading back to the airport.

## A Note on Royal Belfast

If your trip allows an extra stop, Royal Belfast Golf Club in Holywood, overlooking Belfast Lough, is Ireland's oldest golf club (founded 1881) and a Harry Colt parkland design. It works well as an add-on for anyone combining the coastal circuit with a night in Belfast before flying home.

---

## Routing and Logistics

Belfast's two airports (George Best City Airport and Belfast International) are both sensible entry points. A logical three-day routing looks like this:

- **Day 1:** Fly in, head south to Newcastle, play Royal County Down
- **Day 2:** Drive along the coast to Ardglass
- **Day 3:** Cross to the Ards Peninsula for Kirkistown, then return to Belfast

This keeps driving manageable (none of the legs exceeds 90 minutes) and lets the courses build on each other rather than feeling like a random list.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

County Down's golf coast is one of the most concentrated stretches of good links golf available to a UK visitor without getting on a plane to somewhere far-flung. Royal County Down is the draw, and it earns the reputation. But the circuit only really makes sense if you stay for the other two. Ardglass will surprise you. Kirkistown will make you wish more people knew about it.

Book Royal County Down well in advance, check visitor days carefully on the club's own website, and do not assume any third-party pricing you find online is current. Everything else is straightforward.
