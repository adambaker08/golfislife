## The Case for Combining Kintyre and Islay

Most golfers who make it to Machrihanish do so as a standalone mission. A long drive, a couple of rounds, a drive back. That's understandable given how far it sits from most of Scotland's population, but it misses the point. The Kintyre and Islay golf circuit is one of the most rewarding and underplayed links combinations in Britain, and treating the two destinations as a single trip rather than separate pilgrimages transforms the effort-to-reward ratio considerably.

Four courses. A short ferry crossing. A few nights in one of the quieter corners of the country. It takes a bit of planning, but this is exactly the kind of trip you'll talk about for a long time.

---

## Getting to Kintyre: The Honest Logistics

Nobody is going to pretend Kintyre is easy to reach, and there's no point soft-pedalling it. From Glasgow, the drive is roughly four hours along the A83, which winds through Loch Lomond and Argyll before eventually tracking down the spine of the peninsula. It's a scenic route, but a long one.

The alternative is to catch the CalMac ferry from Ardrossan to Campbeltown, which cuts a significant chunk off the road distance and swaps some driving time for a crossing on the Firth of Clyde. Worth checking current CalMac timetables before you build an itinerary around it.

Once you're on the peninsula, the three courses are all within easy reach of each other. A two or three-night base near Machrihanish covers the lot without much faff.

## The Kintyre Courses

### Machrihanish Golf Club

Founded in 1876 and redesigned by Old Tom Morris in 1879, Machrihanish is the reason most golfers come here in the first place. The opening hole is one of the most photographed and talked-about in British golf: a drive across the Atlantic beach, the line of your choosing, with the carry depending on your nerve and the conditions. It is, in the best sense, properly old links golf.

The course sits on ground that was plainly always meant to be a golf course, and it shows. There is no sense of anything being forced or contrived. It's widely considered one of the great natural links in the British Isles, and it's hard to argue with that reputation once you've walked it.

### Machrihanish Dunes

Opened in 2009 and designed by David McLay Kidd (who grew up on Kintyre), Machrihanish Dunes holds a distinction that appears to be genuinely unique: it is the only course in the world built on a Site of Specific Scientific Interest (SSSI). That designation shaped the entire design philosophy. The course works around and with the protected landscape rather than against it, which gives it a feel distinct from any manufactured links experience.

On-site accommodation is available at the resort, which makes it a practical base for the Kintyre leg of the trip. Booking details are on the Machrihanish Dunes website directly.

### Dunaverty Golf Club

About 15 minutes south of Machrihanish in the village of Southend, Dunaverty is the understated third member of this grouping. An 18-hole par-66 natural links, it overlooks Sanda Island and Ailsa Craig, with the Ayrshire coast visible across the water and Northern Ireland appearing on clear days to the southwest. It tends to get far less attention than its neighbours, which is part of its appeal. The mix of holes suits a range of handicaps, and the setting alone is worth the short drive down the peninsula.

## Adding Islay: The Ferry to The Machrie

From Kennacraig, just south of Tarbert on the Kintyre peninsula, CalMac runs car ferries to Islay. The crossing to Port Ellen takes around two hours and ten minutes. There's also a Loganair flight from Glasgow to Islay in roughly 45 minutes if you'd rather leave the car behind for the island leg.

A word of warning on the ferry: car spaces on the Kennacraig to Islay route fill up well in advance during summer. Book early. Foot passengers have more flexibility and can generally buy tickets on the day, but if you're bringing a car, don't leave it to chance.

### The Machrie

The original course on Islay dates to 1891, a Willie Campbell design. It was fully rebuilt in 2018 by David J. Russell to modern championship standard, now stretching over 7,000 yards from the back tees. It appears in Golf Monthly's Top 100 UK and Ireland courses, and the setting in the dunes above the beach on the west coast of the island is as atmospheric as links golf gets.

The resort (operated as Another Place, The Machrie) includes hotel accommodation, a six-hole short course called the Wee Course, a putting course named The Hebrides, a short-game area and a Trackman simulator. It closes for a winter period, historically from around mid-November to mid-February. Check current dates before booking, as the exact window can vary.

The Machrie is also a sensible launchpad for Islay's distilleries. Laphroaig, Lagavulin and Ardbeg are all nearby, which makes the island well worth an extra night for non-golfing companions or a rest day of your own.

## Putting the Circuit Together

A workable structure for the trip might look like this: two nights based near Machrihanish to cover the three Kintyre courses, then a ferry across to Islay for two nights at The Machrie. Five days in total, four rounds, and a part of Scotland that most golfers have heard of but far fewer have actually played.

The effort involved in getting to this corner of the country is real, but that effort is also, in part, why the courses feel the way they do. This isn't a commuter links. It rewards the people who make the journey, and it tends to stick with them.
