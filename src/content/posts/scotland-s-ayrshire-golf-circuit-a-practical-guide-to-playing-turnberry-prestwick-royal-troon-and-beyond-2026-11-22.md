The stretch of Ayrshire coastline southwest of Glasgow is, by any reasonable measure, the most concentrated cluster of Open Championship links in the world. Prestwick, Royal Troon and Turnberry's Ailsa Course between them have hosted more than 35 Opens. And yet most visiting golfers either book one marquee round and leave, or take one look at the green fees and retreat to easier terrain. Neither approach does the region justice.

This guide maps the full Ayrshire golf circuit: the flagship three, the genuinely strong alternatives, and the practical logistics that make or break the trip.

## The Flagship Three

### Prestwick Golf Club

Prestwick is where it all started. The club was formally organised in 1851, with significant input from Old Tom Morris in shaping the course, and the first Open Championship was held here in 1860. It has hosted 24 Opens in total, more than any other venue, though it has not been on the rotation since 1925 due to crowd management limitations.

That history is baked into every corner of the place. Some holes run alongside the railway line. The Cardinal bunker is vast and unforgiving. The routing feels ancient in a way that newer links courses can only imitate.

Visitors are welcome on most weekdays but not on Saturdays, which are reserved for members. If your trip includes a Saturday, plan accordingly.

### Royal Troon

Royal Troon most recently hosted the Open in 2024, when Xander Schauffele won his first major. It offers visitor tee times on Mondays, Tuesdays, and Thursdays only. A handicap certificate is advisable before you book, and you should expect to pay premium rates well into the hundreds of pounds per round at peak times. The famous Postage Stamp, the par-three 8th, is as demanding in real life as its reputation suggests.

Book early. Visitor slots on permitted days fill up months in advance, particularly around the main summer season.

### Turnberry (Ailsa Course)

The Ailsa Course at Turnberry has hosted four Opens and is probably the most visually dramatic of the three, with Ailsa Craig sitting on the horizon across the Firth of Clyde on clear days. The Trump Turnberry resort hotel sits adjacent to the course. The Kintyre Course is also available to visitors and offers a more accessible (and more affordable) entry point to the resort.

Again, book well in advance. This is a destination resort rather than a traditional members' club, so the booking process is more straightforward, but availability at peak times is not generous.

## The Underrated Alternatives

If the flagship trio is out of reach for one or more days of your trip, whether due to access days, budget, or simply wanting more variety, Ayrshire offers a genuinely strong second tier.

### Western Gailes

A few miles from Prestwick Airport, Western Gailes is a proper links course that serves as an Open Qualifying venue when the Championship is held at Troon or Turnberry. That alone tells you the standard is serious. It is a more accessible option in terms of visitor policy and cost, without feeling like a consolation prize.

### Dundonald Links

Based in North Ayrshire, Dundonald has built a solid reputation as both a practical central base for multi-day trips and a course worth playing in its own right. It hosted the Women's Scottish Open in 2024 and 2025. The clubhouse was named best in Scotland in 2025. Visitor access is more flexible than the marquee clubs, which matters when you are trying to piece together a coherent week.

### Kilmarnock (Barassie) Golf Club

Just outside Troon, Barassie offers 27 holes of links golf and operates on something of a word-of-mouth basis among regular Ayrshire visitors. It is the kind of place locals point you towards after you have ticked off the obvious names.

### Prestwick St Nicholas Golf Club

Entirely separate from Prestwick Golf Club and often overlooked as a result of that name confusion, Prestwick St Nicholas was also founded in 1851 and sits with views out over the Ayrshire coast. Worth knowing about if you are spending a couple of days in Prestwick town itself.

### Gailes Links

Another link in the corridor between Troon and the airport, Gailes is cited regularly by people who know the area as offering solid links golf at a sensible price point.

## Practical Logistics

**Getting there:** Glasgow International Airport is the main gateway. Central Ayrshire, around Troon, is roughly 40 minutes by road.

**Getting around:** A hire car is essentially non-negotiable. The courses are spread along the coast with limited public transport connections between them, and you will likely be carrying clubs.

**Where to base yourself:** Troon town works well as a central hub. Prestwick town is a quieter alternative with an authentic feel, comparable in some ways to St Andrews but considerably less crowded. Turnberry is worth a night if the budget stretches, particularly for the lighthouse walk, which is a worthwhile detour even on a golf trip.

**Walking:** Traditional links courses here expect you to walk. Buggies are generally restricted to golfers with a medical certificate. Factor this in if you are planning four or five rounds in quick succession.

**Handicap certificates:** Carry one. Royal Troon explicitly asks for it, and other clubs may request evidence of playing standard.

## Building a Realistic Itinerary

A three-night, four-round trip is about right for a first visit. One round each at Prestwick and Royal Troon (on a permitted visitor day), one at Western Gailes or Dundonald, and a fourth day either at the Ailsa or as a buffer for weather, which in Ayrshire is an entirely sensible precaution.

If budget is a constraint, build around the second tier entirely. Western Gailes, Dundonald, Barassie and Prestwick St Nicholas would make for a cracking four-round trip at a fraction of the cost, and none of them would leave you feeling you had settled.

Ayrshire rewards people who do the planning properly. The courses are all there, the access is manageable with a bit of research, and the coastline is as good as links golf gets anywhere in the world.
