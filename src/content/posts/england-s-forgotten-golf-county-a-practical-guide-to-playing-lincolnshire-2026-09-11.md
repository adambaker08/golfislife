Mention Lincolnshire to most golfers and you'll get a blank look, maybe a vague association with flat land and caravans. That's a mistake worth exploiting. England's forgotten golf county quietly holds one of the finest heathland courses in the country, a genuinely old-school links that most people have never heard of, and a James Braid coastal design with rooms attached. Put those together over two or three nights and you have a trip that compares favourably with far more fashionable destinations at a fraction of the price and fuss.

Here's how to put it together.

## The Anchor: Woodhall Spa and the Hotchkin

Any serious Lincolnshire trip is built around Woodhall Spa. The National Golf Centre, home of England Golf, sits about 20 miles from Lincoln and offers two courses: the Bracken, which is a solid round in its own right, and the Hotchkin, which is the reason you're making the journey.

The Hotchkin ranks inside Golf Monthly's UK and Ireland Top 100 (12th at the time of writing) and earned that position honestly. A recent renovation by architect Tom Doak has sharpened its heathland character, restoring the kind of sandy, atmospheric feel that makes the best Surrey and Berkshire courses so compelling. The Hotchkin is not famous the way Wentworth is famous, but it's a better test than many courses that carry more name recognition.

**Practical details for the Hotchkin:** A handicap limit applies (24 for men, 36 for ladies), so bring your handicap certificate or CDH number. England Golf-affiliated members receive a discounted green fee, which is worth knowing if you're a club member in England. Advance booking is required and there's a non-refundable deposit of £20 per golfer. Book online or by phone directly through the club. Specific green fee rates are best confirmed with the club at the time of booking, as pricing can change seasonally.

Woodhall Spa village itself is a pleasant base. It has a Victorian-spa-town character, is quiet without being dull, and keeps you close to the course for an early tee time without a stressful drive.

## The Coastal Leg: Seacroft at Skegness

After Woodhall Spa, move east to the coast. Seacroft Golf Club at Skegness is the kind of course that rewards golfers who seek things out rather than following the crowd. Founded in 1895 and sitting alongside the Gibraltar Point Nature Reserve (a Site of Special Scientific Interest), it's a proper links: out-and-back layout, 75 bunkers, narrow hogsback fairways, and a coastal wind that turns a modest card into a severe examination.

At 6,492 yards and par 71, it's not a monster by modern standards, but the conditions rather than the length are what bite. Fast-draining sandy soil means the course plays almost year-round on normal tees and greens, which matters if you're planning a shoulder-season trip. Lincolnshire is one of the drier parts of England, which helps.

National Club Golfer rated Seacroft sixth for value out of 250 clubs across Great Britain and Ireland, which tells you something about how the green fee compares to the quality on offer. Indicative figures from 2023 put weekday fees around £60 and weekend fees around £70 in high season, but these should be treated as a rough guide only. Contact the club directly for current pricing before booking. A handicap certificate is required and advance booking is strongly advised.

## A Stay-and-Play Option: North Shore Hotel and Golf Club

If you'd rather not shuttle between accommodation and the coast, North Shore Hotel and Golf Club in Skegness solves the problem neatly. James Braid designed the original course in 1910, and the layout mixes links and parkland holes across 6,216 yards (par 71), with views across the Wash towards Norfolk.

The hotel on site has 34 en-suite rooms, a bar, and a restaurant, so you can roll in off the course without needing to find anywhere else. It's open to visitors for casual rounds. Fees quoted by third-party sources are not current or confirmed, so contact North Shore directly for up-to-date pricing and availability.

## How to Stitch the Trip Together

A logical structure for two nights: arrive in the Lincoln or Woodhall Spa area, play the Hotchkin on day one (the Bracken on day two if you want a second round in the area), then move east to Skegness for the coastal portion. Playing Seacroft and North Shore on the same or consecutive days makes geographic sense, and the drive between Woodhall Spa and Skegness is comfortably under an hour.

Lincoln itself is worth an evening for non-golfers in the group. The cathedral and castle make it one of England's more underrated city stops.

East Midlands Airport is a reasonable arrival point if you're flying. Road access from both north and south is straightforward.

## The Genuine Takeaway

Lincolnshire's appeal as a golf destination rests on exactly the things that get overlooked: no hype, no waiting lists built on reputation alone, serious course quality, and a dry climate that keeps conditions reasonable across a long season. If you're after a UK golf break that doesn't involve fighting for a tee time at a course you've seen on television, this is a corner of England worth putting in the diary.
