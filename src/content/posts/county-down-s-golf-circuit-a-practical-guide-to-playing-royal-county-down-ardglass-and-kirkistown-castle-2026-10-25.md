Most UK golfers who make the trip to County Down do exactly the same thing: they book a round on the Championship Links at Royal County Down, spend the next few days in quiet awe, and head home wishing they'd stayed longer. A few even wonder whether one round justified the journey. The answer is to stop treating it as a pilgrimage to a single shrine and start treating County Down as a proper three-course links circuit. Royal County Down is the centrepiece, no question, but Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle are genuine rounds in their own right, not just ways to fill the days between arrival and departure.

This guide covers the practical details: what each course is like, what it will cost, and how to put the trip together sensibly.

---

## Royal County Down: The One Everyone Comes For

Royal County Down (RCD) in Newcastle sits against the Mountains of Mourne on the edge of Murlough Nature Reserve, and the setting alone would make it worth visiting even if the golf were merely decent. The golf is not merely decent. The Championship Links is a par 71 measuring around 7,186 yards from the back tees, and it consistently appears in the top five links courses in the world by any ranking you care to name. Founded in 1889, it has the kind of course character that photographs can only partially convey: blind tee shots with aiming posts to guide you, deep bunkers whose lips are laced with heather and marram grass, fast domed greens, and fairways pinched by purple heather and golden gorse on both sides.

If you have not played it before, take a forecaddie. The blind shots in particular make local knowledge genuinely useful rather than just nice to have, and the forecaddie fee (approximately £110 per group, paid in cash directly after the round) is fair for what you get. There is no ATM at the club, so pick up cash in Newcastle town beforehand.

**Visitor days** on the Championship Links are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday. No play on Wednesday or Saturday. The 2026 green fee is £450 per round. That is a significant sum, and it is non-negotiable, but it is in line with the top tier of British and Irish links. Booking is via the club's enquiry form, not a ballot, but availability is limited and early enquiry is strongly advised. The club also has the shorter Annesley Links (roughly par 66 equivalent, £120 per round), available Monday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday, which is worth considering if your group wants a second day at RCD without the full Championship fee.

Dress code is standard but firmly enforced: collared shirts, no denim, no cargo shorts, soft-spiked shoes. Hats come off indoors.

For accommodation, the Slieve Donard Resort and Spa is walking distance from the first tee. The Burrendale Hotel is around a mile away and more modestly priced. Both are popular with golf visitors, so book alongside your tee time rather than after.

## Ardglass: Clifftop Golf With a 15th-Century Clubhouse

Ardglass Golf Club sits on the north-east coast of County Down, roughly 30 minutes by road from Newcastle. Founded in 1896, it is a par 70 measuring 6,268 yards. What makes it immediately distinctive is the setting: the Irish Sea comes into play on approximately eight holes, and you have sea views from virtually everywhere on the course. The second hole, a par 3 played over a gorge, is the kind of thing that gets remembered.

The clubhouse is worth noting for its own sake. Part of it occupies a fortified building with sections dating to 1405, making it among the oldest structures used as a golf clubhouse anywhere. It is a properly unusual place to have a post-round pint.

Green fees at Ardglass are in the range of roughly £115 to £200 depending on day and season, but fees vary and the figures in circulation are not fully consistent. Verify current rates directly with the club before booking. Do not arrive with a fixed number in mind.

Ardglass works well either as the round you play the day before RCD (a good way to find your links legs before the Championship course demands them) or as the round the day after (a gentler, cheaper, but still genuinely enjoyable way to end the trip on a high rather than a flat anti-climax).

## Kirkistown Castle: The Underrated One

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club at Cloughey, on the eastern shore of the Ards Peninsula, is the one most visitors skip entirely. That is a mistake. Founded in 1902 and redesigned by James Braid in 1934, it is a compact, quirky links with a sandy base that keeps it playable year-round. At par 70 and 6,167 yards, it is not a long course, but it plays with a character that rewards careful driving. Several of the greens are elevated (the 2nd, 8th, 10th, 12th, and 16th among them), the rough punishes loose shots, and the two nine-hole loops both return to the clubhouse, which makes the layout feel old-fashioned in exactly the right way.

Visitor green fees are broadly in the £35 to £50 range for most rounds, though exact current pricing should be confirmed with the club. It is open to visitors seven days a week. For a circuit that already includes £450 at RCD, having a course this good at this price point is genuinely useful.

## Putting the Circuit Together

The three courses do not form a neat triangle, but they are manageable as a two-night or three-night stay. Newcastle (RCD and Ardglass are both within 30 miles of Belfast) works as a base. Kirkistown is on the Ards Peninsula, roughly 45 minutes by road from Ardglass, so a three-day circuit could work as Kirkistown one day, RCD the next, Ardglass the third, all staying in Newcastle.

A hire car is essential. There are no practical public transport links between the courses. Getting to Northern Ireland from Great Britain is straightforward: ferry from Cairnryan or Liverpool, or fly into Belfast International or George Best Belfast City Airport. Northern Ireland uses pound sterling, so no currency exchange is needed.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

Royal County Down at £450 is expensive, and it should be. It is one of the great links courses, and the fee reflects that. But if you are making the journey from mainland Britain, you are spending money on flights or a ferry, accommodation, and travel anyway. Spread the cost and the experience across three courses and the trip suddenly becomes genuinely worthwhile rather than an expensive day out with a long commute. Ardglass and Kirkistown are not consolation prizes. They are good golf courses that happen to sit next to one of the best in the world.
