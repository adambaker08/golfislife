East Lothian doesn't need you to discover it. Golfers who know their links know it well. What it does need is better planning — because too many visitors book the headline names, underestimate the logistics, and leave feeling they've only half-played it.

The region officially carries the name Scotland's Golf Coast, and the label is earned. More than 21 courses sit within roughly 30 miles of coastline, all within an hour of Edinburgh Airport. The density of championship-standard links here is genuinely unusual. The practical question is how to use that concentration sensibly.

---

## The Big Two — And Why They Take Work

**Muirfield** and **North Berwick West Links** sit at the top of most golfers' lists, and for good reason. Both feature in Golf Digest's World's 100 Greatest Courses as of 2025 — Muirfield at sixth, North Berwick at ninth.

Muirfield's concentric-loop layout is unlike almost anything else: the outer nine runs clockwise, the inner counter-clockwise, which means the wind comes at you from a different direction on almost every hole. Visitor access is strictly limited to two days a week, and tee times compete for hard. If Muirfield is a priority, you book it first and build the rest of the trip around the date you get.

North Berwick West is the more immediately lovable course — quirky, historic, with stone walls crossing fairways and a run of short holes that have influenced course designers worldwide. The fourth, a short par-3 known as the Redan, has been replicated at courses from Shinnecock Hills to Australia. The setting, right against the town beach, is as good as links golf gets.

Both will fill up fast in peak season. If you're planning a trip for summer 2026 or beyond — when interest in Scottish golf is likely to rise ahead of The Open returning to St Andrews in July 2027 — book as early as you can.

---

## Gullane: The One That Deserves More Credit

**Gullane No.1** hosted the Scottish Open and offers one of the best elevated views in links golf from its seventh hole, looking out across the Firth of Forth. It tends to get overshadowed by Muirfield (which sits just up the road), but it's a proper championship test and generally easier to get on.

Gullane runs three courses in total, which makes it a practical base for a multi-day trip. Nos. 2 and 3 are shorter and less demanding — useful options if you want to mix difficulty across your itinerary.

---

## The Courses Worth Adding if You Have Time

**The Renaissance Club** is the most modern course in the region, a pure championship layout that has hosted the Genesis Scottish Open. Less quirky than the old links, but precise and demanding.

**Dunbar** sits at the eastern end of the region and is often left off itineraries simply because of distance. It's a championship-standard course at a more accessible green fee than the headliners — worth the extra drive if you have a spare half-day.

**Kilspindie** is the low-key one. Set hard beside the water, short by modern standards, but an honest links with character. It's the sort of course you enjoy without quite knowing why, and the green fees are among the lowest in the area — broadly in the range of £50–60, though you should confirm current rates before booking as fees change seasonally.

**Craigielaw** is newer, less scenically dramatic, but has solid practice facilities and is often available when others aren't. A good warm-up round at the start of a trip.

## What to Expect on the Ground

**Getting there:** Edinburgh Airport is the obvious entry point — the drive to the eastern end of the region takes about an hour. The courses are strung along the coast rather than clustered in one spot, so a hire car or minibus is effectively essential. Public transport links exist between some of the towns, but they're too slow and infrequent to work around tee times.

**The weather:** East Lothian reportedly sits in a coastal microclimate that makes it drier than much of the Scottish interior — that's a reasonable working assumption, not a guarantee. Pack for all conditions regardless. Wind is the constant variable; on a calm day these courses play long, and on a typical day they play significantly longer.

**Green fees:** Costs across the region span a wide range. Based on figures current at time of research, the rough order runs from Kilspindie at the lower end up through Dunbar, Gullane No.1, North Berwick West, The Renaissance Club, and Muirfield at the top (broadly £395 for visitors at the time of writing). These figures change seasonally and should be confirmed directly with each club before you book — treat any numbers you see online as an indication rather than a price.

## A Realistic Itinerary

Three days gives you a good trip. Five days is when you start to feel like you've actually played the region. A workable structure:

- **Day 1:** Arrive, play Kilspindie or Craigielaw — get your eye in, shake off the travel
- **Day 2:** North Berwick West Links
- **Day 3:** Gullane No.1 (or Muirfield if you have the tee time)
- **Days 4–5:** Dunbar, Renaissance Club, or the second Gullane course

---

## The Takeaway

East Lothian rewards golfers who plan methodically rather than just chasing the famous names. Muirfield and North Berwick are worth the effort and cost — but the trip becomes something better when you give time to Gullane, Dunbar, and the quirkier options in between. Sort your Muirfield and North Berwick dates first, fill the rest around them, and don't arrive expecting quiet — this is a well-travelled stretch of coastline that other golfers have already found.
