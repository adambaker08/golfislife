## More Than a Pilgrimage to One Course

County Down has a habit of being reduced to a single conversation: Royal County Down, world top five, bucket list, expensive, done. Which is a shame, because the county sitting between Belfast and the Mourne Mountains quietly holds one of the best value-for-effort links circuits anywhere in the British Isles. Play Royal County Down, yes, but pair it with Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle and you have a trip that earns its ferry crossing several times over.

This guide treats all three as a coherent itinerary rather than treating the famous one as the headline act and the others as an afterthought.

---

## Getting There

No passport is required travelling from Great Britain to Northern Ireland, which removes one layer of planning. For most UK golfers, the ferry is the practical choice if you want to bring clubs without the faff of airline baggage fees.

Stena Line runs from Cairnryan in southwest Scotland to Belfast, roughly two hours and fifteen minutes, with up to six sailings a day. P&O Ferries operates a similar Cairnryan to Larne route at around two hours. If you are travelling from the Midlands or north of England, Stena also runs an overnight Liverpool (Birkenhead) to Belfast crossing of around eight hours, with cabins available, which means you arrive rested and ready to play.

Flying is straightforward too. Belfast City Airport (George Best) is served from many UK airports and sits around fifty to sixty-five minutes from Newcastle on the County Down coast.

From Belfast to Newcastle, where Royal County Down is located, is roughly thirty to thirty-five miles south: a forty-five to fifty minute drive. Ardglass is a further twenty to thirty minutes from Newcastle. Kirkistown Castle, on the Ards Peninsula near Cloughey, is about forty-five minutes from Belfast, making it sensible to play on arrival day or departure day.

## Royal County Down: the One Everyone Comes For

It deserves the reputation. The Championship Links sits between the Mourne Mountains and the Irish Sea within Murlough Nature Reserve, and on a clear morning it is as visually dramatic as links golf gets. Par 71, just over 7,100 yards from the back tees, and with blind shots and deep rough that will punish optimism.

**Practical booking notes.** Visitor days are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and selected Sunday times. Groups must be four-balls, and crucially, all groups are required to include at least one caddie. This is non-negotiable, not merely a polite suggestion. Take it seriously and book a caddie at the same time as your tee time.

The 2026 peak-season green fee for the Championship Links is £450 per person, with the Annesley Links (a shorter, redesigned companion course) at £120. Fees for 2027 are confirmed at £495 and £150 respectively. That makes RCD the most expensive course in GB and Ireland at the time of writing, ahead of St Andrews Old Course and Muirfield. Full payment is required at booking and is non-refundable unless the club cancels the round due to weather. Plan accordingly.

Book as far in advance as you can manage. These slots fill well ahead of season.

**Staying nearby.** The Slieve Donard Resort and Spa in Newcastle sits directly adjacent to the course, with the beach on one side and the golf course on the other. Four dining options, a spa, and a history that matches the surroundings. Worth knowing about when you are arranging accommodation.

## Ardglass: Clifftop Links with a Clubhouse Unlike Any Other

Ardglass Golf Club is about thirty minutes from Newcastle and a course that regularly gets underplayed on the County Down circuit. Founded in 1896 and extended to eighteen holes in 1970, it runs along the clifftops above the Irish Sea. The sea is visible from every hole and comes directly into play on eight of them, which keeps your attention.

The clubhouse occupies a fortified building dating to 1405, recorded as one of the oldest golf clubhouses in the world. That alone is worth knowing before you walk in.

The course plays to par 70 at 6,268 yards. Visitors are welcome, and it is typically more accessible in terms of booking than RCD. On green fees: the figures in circulation vary quite a bit across different sources, so contact the club directly before you budget. What is consistent is that it sits well below Royal County Down's rates.

## Kirkistown Castle: the Quiet One That Deserves Better

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club on the Ards Peninsula is often described as the most easterly eighteen-hole course in Ireland and the nearest links to Belfast at around forty-five minutes by road. Founded in 1902 and shaped by James Braid in a 1934 redesign, it plays to par 70 across terrain that rewards ground game and penalises aerial ambition.

The course has been recognised as the best value golf experience in Ulster, and it was recently reaffirmed with awards to that effect. Approximate green fees quoted by various guides suggest it is very accessible, but again, confirm directly with the club before you book. These things shift.

One small piece of history worth knowing: near the first tee is a feature called The Dip, a depression in the ground that dates to World War Two airfield construction on the site. It is the kind of detail that makes a course feel like it has a life beyond the scorecard.

Visitors are welcome every day, which gives you flexibility that RCD does not.

## Putting the Circuit Together

A sensible three-night itinerary might look like this: arrive via ferry, play Kirkistown Castle on the way down to Newcastle. Play Royal County Down on day two (having booked months ahead). Play Ardglass on day three before heading back north to catch the ferry.

That gives you three distinct links experiences, each with its own character, without any round becoming a consolation prize. The total outlay for golf remains significant given RCD's fee, but Ardglass and Kirkistown add genuine quality rather than padding.

County Down's golf circuit rewards the traveller who bothers to look past the headline. Do that, and it is one of the strongest three-course trips available from mainland Britain, full stop.
