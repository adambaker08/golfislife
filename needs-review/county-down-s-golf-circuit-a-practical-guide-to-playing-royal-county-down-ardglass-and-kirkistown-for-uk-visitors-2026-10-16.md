## The Case for Going Beyond One Course

Most UK golfers who make the crossing to County Down have one thing on their mind: Royal County Down. And fair enough. It consistently ranks among the top five courses in the world, sits beneath the Mourne Mountains on the edge of Dundrum Bay, and delivers the kind of golf that genuinely stays with you. But building a trip around a single round, however good, is a missed opportunity. County Down's golf circuit, taken properly, includes two other courses that more than justify the journey on their own merits. This guide maps out how to do the whole thing sensibly.

## Royal County Down: What You're Actually Getting Into

Founded in 1889 and stretching to 7,186 yards off the back tees, the Championship Links at Royal County Down (RCD) is a par 71 with towering sand dunes, gorse-covered sandhills, and a number of blind tee shots that catch first-timers out. The club also has the Annesley Links, a shorter par 66 course that's an excellent option for a second-day warm-up or a wind-down round if legs or budget dictate something gentler.

Getting a tee time on the Championship Course is not straightforward. Visitor access is restricted to certain days and time slots, Monday, Tuesday, Thursday mornings, Friday, and Sunday afternoons being the typical windows (though this is subject to change and should be confirmed directly with the club). Demand is high, so book as far ahead as you possibly can.

On green fees: reliable third-party sources, including National Club Golfer's 2026 survey, suggest peak-season rates on the Championship Course are in the region of £450 per round. That figure has climbed sharply in recent years and varies by month and day. Do not assume any number you read online is current. Check royalcountydown.org before booking, and budget conservatively. The Annesley Links is a fraction of that cost and well worth including.

A local caddie is strongly recommended, particularly on the Championship Course. The blind shots are manageable once you know where you're going, and a caddie who reads the wind off the Mournes is worth every penny.

## Ardglass: The Clifftop Course That Earns Its Reputation

About 20 miles north of Newcastle sits Ardglass Golf Club, a course that has moved from relative obscurity to a firm place on the County Down itinerary. The clubhouse reportedly dates to the 13th century, and the routing covers nearly two miles of jagged rocky cliffs above the sea. It is dramatic in the most honest sense of the word, not manufactured drama but the genuine kind that comes from playing exposed links golf on a clifftop promontory.

Green fees at Ardglass have risen to reflect its growing profile. Peak season rates in 2025 were reported around £220, though again, check directly with the club for current pricing. It is not a cheap day out, but by the standards of what it delivers, most golfers find it worthwhile.

If you are routing a three-or-four-day trip, Ardglass pairs naturally with RCD as a second-day round while you are still based in Newcastle, before moving on.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Ards Peninsula's Best-Kept Secret

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club sits on the eastern shore of the Ards Peninsula near Cloughey, roughly 45 minutes by road from Belfast. It is the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland, founded in 1902 and shaped by James Braid in 1934. At around 6,167 yards and playing to par 69-70, it is not the longest course you will ever play, but the quick-draining links terrain and exposure to the Irish Sea make it a proper examination, particularly in any kind of wind.

Green fees are in the £35 to £40 range, which makes it one of the better-value links experiences anywhere in the UK and Ireland. It has been recognised as the Best Value Golf Experience in Ulster. The signature hole is the par-4 10th, approximately 435 yards with a raised pulpit green, which has a strong claim to being one of the harder par-4s in Irish golf. Kirkistown also has the practical advantage of being rarely closed after heavy rain, making it a reliable choice if you are travelling in shoulder season.

## How to Route the Trip

A car is not optional here. County Down's courses are spread across a coastal arc and there is no practical public transport link between them.

A sensible four-day structure looks like this:

**Day 1.** Arrive, settle into Newcastle. An evening walk along the seafront to get your bearings is not wasted time.

**Day 2.** Championship Links at Royal County Down. Take the caddie. Allow time afterwards to recover.

**Day 3.** Annesley Links in the morning (good for the legs and the wallet), then drive to Ardglass in the afternoon or the following morning.

**Day 4.** Drive north and east to Kirkistown on the Ards Peninsula before heading back to Belfast for departure.

Newcastle is the natural base for the first half of the trip. The Slieve Donard Resort and Spa sits immediately adjacent to RCD and is the obvious choice for those who want convenience and a certain level of comfort. It is not a budget option, but the location is hard to argue with.

## Getting There from the UK Mainland

Belfast is served by two airports. George Best Belfast City Airport (BHD) is the more convenient option for County Down, roughly 30 to 40 minutes by car to Newcastle or Downpatrick. Belfast International (BFS) adds another 30 to 45 minutes of driving.

For those travelling with their own clubs and a car, the ferry is worth serious consideration. Stena Line and P&O Ferries run services from Cairnryan in southwest Scotland to Belfast, and there is also a Liverpool to Belfast route. Crossing overnight on the Liverpool sailing in particular is a practical and reasonably comfortable option that avoids airline baggage charges and the indignity of travelling bag fees for a golf travel case.

Northern Ireland uses pounds sterling, the same as the rest of the UK, so no currency change is needed.

## The Honest Takeaway

This is not a cheap trip, particularly once RCD green fees are factored in. But County Down's golf circuit offers something that is genuinely hard to find: world-class links golf within a short drive of each other, easily reachable without flying, in a part of the world that remains less overrun than comparable destinations in Scotland or even southwest England. Kirkistown alone, at its price point, is reason enough to extend the trip by a day. Book RCD early, plan your route before you leave, and do not skip Ardglass on the assumption that one great course is enough.
