## Why County Down Deserves a Dedicated Trip

Northern Ireland has quietly become one of the most compelling golf destinations in the British Isles. Royal Portrush gets plenty of the headlines, but County Down's golf circuit — anchored by Royal County Down, with Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle on either side — offers a genuinely distinct experience and, crucially, a range of budgets that suits more than just the expense-account golfer.

The logistics are straightforward enough. Belfast has two airports, and if you're coming from mainland Britain with a car and a full bag, the ferry from Cairnryan in Scotland (roughly two hours twenty minutes sailing) or the overnight crossing from Liverpool are both worth considering. A base in or near Newcastle puts you within thirty minutes of Royal County Down and an hour or so of the other two.

## Royal County Down: Worth Every Penny, But Plan Carefully

No conversation about this corner of Ireland starts anywhere other than Royal County Down. Founded in 1889, the Championship Links runs to 7,186 yards at par 71, set between the Mountains of Mourne and Dundrum Bay. It sits within the Murlough Nature Reserve, and the layout zigzags back and forth across the duneland in a way that means the views shift constantly throughout the round. It has been ranked consistently among the top five courses in the world, and that reputation is not overinflated.

The practical reality for visiting golfers, though, is that a round here requires advance planning and a reasonable budget. Peak-season green fees on the Championship Links are around £450 per round based on figures reported for 2026, though you should check the current rate directly with the club at royalcountydown.org before booking. Visitor days are typically Monday, Tuesday, Thursday morning, Friday, and Sunday afternoon. Wednesday is not available to visitors (again, confirm directly with the club before travelling). A handicap certificate is required: the figure cited by most sources is 24 for men and 36 for ladies, but verify this on the club's own website as the authoritative source.

If the Championship Links fee is out of reach, or you simply want an extra round on the same ground, the club also offers the Annesley Links (par 66, 4,548 yards). Guide prices cited by multiple sources put this in the low-to-mid £40s range — considerably more accessible, and still a proper links experience. Treat that figure as indicative and check with the club.

The Slieve Donard Resort and Spa in Newcastle is the obvious base for anyone playing RCD, sitting almost within chipping distance of the first tee. No affiliate arrangement is in place here, so that's purely an informational mention rather than a recommendation with a financial interest behind it.

## Ardglass: Dramatic Cliffs, Ancient Clubhouse, Honest Green Fees

Around thirty miles south of Belfast and roughly half an hour from Royal County Down, Ardglass Golf Club sits on the Irish Sea coast in a way that makes an impression before you've even teed off. The opening hole is a short uphill par 4 with a tee shot widely described as one of the most dramatic in Irish golf. The front nine hugs cliff edges with the sea constantly in view.

The course runs to approximately 6,268 yards at par 70, and while the yardage is modest, the coastal exposure and terrain make it a proper test. In 2026 rankings, Golf World placed it 165th in Great Britain and Ireland, and Irish Golfer listed it 41st in Ireland. Guide prices cited by UK-facing sources are around £115 midweek and £135 to £140 at weekends — a significant saving over Royal County Down, though fees have risen sharply as the course's reputation has spread. As with all the courses here, check current rates directly with the club before booking.

The clubhouse is Ardglass Castle, parts of which date to 1405. The club describes it as one of the oldest golf clubhouses in the world, and that claim has real substance to it, though rival claimants do exist. Whether or not it takes the title definitively, it's a remarkable building for a mid-round pint. Speaking of which: there is a well-regarded halfway cottage at the rear of the 7th green, a pint-and-sea-views setup that has become something of a calling card for the place.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Most Underrated Links in the Circuit

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club, on the Ards Peninsula near Cloughey, rounds out a County Down golf circuit beautifully and offers the best value of the three. Founded in 1902 and significantly shaped by James Braid in 1934, the course runs to 6,167 yards at par 70 from the white tees. It has recently been recognised as the Best Value Golf Experience in Ulster, and one source quotes green fees at around £40 per 18 holes, though that should be treated as indicative and confirmed with the club.

What makes Kirkistown particularly practical is that visitors are welcome every day (advance booking still required), and as a quick-draining links it is rarely closed after rain, which anyone who has planned a golf trip to this part of the world will know is not a trivial consideration. It is also the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland and, depending on your route in and out of Belfast, likely the closest links to the city at around 45 minutes via the A20 through Newtownards. Practice facilities are solid: a practice ground, two putting greens, a pitching green, and practice nets.

## A Word on Clandeboye

If you are flying into Belfast and want a round to shake off the travel before heading south towards Newcastle, Clandeboye Golf Club near Bangor is a strong option. Its Dufferin Championship course (par 71, 6,742 yards) is a parkland and heathland layout with a serious feel, and guide prices cited by multiple sources sit in the £70 to £90 range midweek. It is not a links, but it is a good warm-up and an easy drive from both Belfast airports.

## Making the Most of the Trip

The honest approach to County Down's golf circuit is to treat Royal County Down as the centrepiece and organise everything else around it. Book RCD well in advance, check visitor day availability on the club's website, and get your handicap certificate in order before you travel. Then build Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle in around it. The contrast between the three courses, in terms of scale, setting, and price, is exactly what makes the circuit work. You get a world-famous links, a cliffside gem with a medieval clubhouse, and a unpretentious, brilliant-value links that most visiting golfers have never heard of.

That is a good trip.
