## The Case for Going North-East

Every year, thousands of golfers make the pilgrimage to Fife or head down to Ayrshire, tick the famous names off their lists, and fly home. Meanwhile, Aberdeenshire sits quietly in the north-east, offering a links roster that is arguably just as compelling and considerably less crowded. This practical guide to playing Aberdeenshire is aimed at golfers travelling from elsewhere in the UK who want to build a proper self-contained trip rather than squeezing a round or two around a work visit to Aberdeen.

The region has more than 50 courses of varying ambition and budget, from world-ranked links to low-key seaside tracks where you can walk on and pay not much more than the cost of a decent pub dinner. The coastline stretches north from the city itself, and within an hour's drive you have some of the most characterful links land in Scotland.

## The Headline Courses

### Royal Aberdeen (Balgownie)

Founded in 1780, Royal Aberdeen is the sixth oldest golf club in the world. The Balgownie links needs no invention to make it sound impressive. The front nine in particular is consistently cited among the finest in golf, running through natural dunes with a rawness that too many modern courses have designed out of existence. Visitor access is available, but it is worth contacting the club well ahead of travel to understand the available windows. This is not a drop-in course.

### Cruden Bay

About 25 miles north of Aberdeen, Cruden Bay is the course that tends to convert the sceptical. Designed by Tom Simpson and Herbert Fowler in 1926, it uses the enormous natural dunes of the Bay of Cruden to create a round full of blind shots, quirky angles, and moments that make you stop and look around. It holds a World Top 100 ranking and earns it.

Weekend visitor access can be restricted, and green fees at top-ranked courses can shift seasonally. Shoulder-season weekdays in May or September are generally regarded as the best combination of value and conditions. Check directly with the club for current availability and pricing before planning around a specific date.

### Murcar Links

Sharing the same dune system as Royal Aberdeen and approximately four minutes' drive from its car park, Murcar is one of those courses that might appear to play second fiddle on reputation alone but absolutely does not on the ground. Crumpled fairways, huge sand dunes, heather, whins, and burns make it a proper test. Originally laid out in 1909 with advice from Archie Simpson, it was ranked 30th in the 2025 Top 100 Scottish Golf Courses. Playing both Murcar and Royal Aberdeen on consecutive days makes for a very satisfying forty-eight hours.

### Trump International, Balmedie

About 16km north of Aberdeen at the Menie Estate, the Hawtree-designed course (opened 2012, measuring 7,428 yards) hosted the DP World Tour's Scottish Championship from 2025. A second course, the Trump New Course, opened in July 2025, designed by Martin Hawtree, Christian Lundin, and Christine Fraser, and was named World's Best Golf Course 2025 by the World Golf Awards. Whatever your view on the name above the clubhouse door, the golf product here is serious and the dune landscape it occupies is extraordinary.

## The Quieter Options Worth Knowing

Not every day needs to be a bucket-list round. Aberdeenshire has quieter and more affordable tracks that give the trip a different rhythm.

**Fraserburgh Golf Club** is one of the oldest clubs in the world, founded in 1777, with the present course opened in 1891 and redesigned by James Braid in 1922. It is an honest links with history you can feel and fees that will not require a second mortgage.

**Royal Tarlair**, further up the coast at Macduff, is a long drive from the city (around 55 minutes) but its 13th hole is widely regarded as one of the more memorable on this coastline, and the course is known for being genuinely affordable.

**Newburgh Links** and **Stonehaven** also come up as value options for those wanting a coastal round without the advance planning a top-100 visit requires.

## Practical Logistics for Playing Aberdeenshire

A car is essential. The courses are spread along the coast, and public transport does not connect them in any useful way. Trains from Edinburgh to Aberdeen take approximately 2.5 hours, after which car hire from the city is the most practical option.

Base yourself in or close to Aberdeen city and you can reach Royal Aberdeen, Murcar, and Trump easily. Cruden Bay is a further 35 minutes north, so a night in Cruden Bay village or nearby works well if you want to play there without an early-morning drive.

The best travel window is late spring through early autumn. Daylight stretches long in the north-east in summer, which means the possibility of genuinely late tee times, and the courses are at their most playable.

Off the course, Slains Castle near Cruden Bay is worth an hour of your afternoon, the castle trail and whisky distilleries give you options on a rest day, and the fishing towns along the coast have a character that feels nothing like a golf resort.

## The Real Takeaway

Aberdeenshire rewards golfers who are willing to plan rather than just show up. Book ahead for the bigger names, leave a day or two flexible for the smaller clubs, and accept that some of the most enjoyable rounds of a trip often happen at a course you almost skipped. The north-east has enough variety, enough quality, and enough elbow room to make a standalone golf trip rather than an afterthought to somewhere more famous.
