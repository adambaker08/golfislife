County Down is one of those destinations that golfers talk about in hushed, slightly reverent tones. Royal County Down sits at the top of Golf Digest's world rankings for 2024, the Mourne Mountains rise behind the fairways, and Dundrum Bay glitters on a clear morning. But the county offers a great deal more than a single famous round, and visitors who fly in, play RCD and fly straight back out are leaving the best part of the trip on the table. County Down's golf circuit, taking in Royal County Down alongside Ardglass and Kirkistown Castle (with an optional fourth stop at Kilkeel), makes a compelling case for three or four days properly spent.

This guide is aimed at golfers based on the mainland UK, working out the logistics honestly rather than making it sound simpler than it is.

---

## Getting There

Northern Ireland is part of the UK, but reaching County Down from most of England, Scotland or Wales still takes some planning. Belfast has two airports worth knowing about.

**George Best Belfast City Airport** is the more convenient of the two, sitting around 30 to 40 minutes from Newcastle (the town, not the English one), where Royal County Down is based. **Belfast International** is around an hour and 15 minutes away from Newcastle. Both airports are served by regular flights from major UK cities, often at reasonable fares.

If you prefer not to fly, the ferry is a genuinely good option, particularly if you want to bring your own clubs without the hassle of airline golf bag fees. Stena Line runs from Cairnryan to Belfast in around two hours and 15 minutes, and P&O Ferries cover the same Cairnryan to Larne crossing in roughly two hours. For those travelling from further south, Stena Line also offers an overnight Liverpool Birkenhead to Belfast sailing of around eight hours, which is a practical way to arrive fresh for a morning round.

Once you're in County Down, a hire car is essentially non-negotiable for this trip. The courses are spread across the peninsula, public transport connections between them are limited, and you will want flexibility to move between venues.

## Royal County Down

The Championship Links at Royal County Down opened in 1889, with contributions over the years from Old Tom Morris, Harry Vardon and Harry Colt. The course is a par-71 set against a backdrop that is, by most accounts, as dramatic as links golf gets anywhere in the world.

A few things to know before you try to book.

Visitor days on the Championship Links are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday afternoons only. Wednesday and Saturday are closed to visitors entirely. There is no online booking portal; all reservations are made by phone or email directly with the club. Tee times for the peak summer months reportedly sell out very quickly once the season's allocation opens in spring, so if you are planning a June to August visit, getting your booking in early is not optional.

Green fees for the Championship Links are indicative at £450 per player for 2026 (verify directly with the club, as rates can change). The secondary Annesley Links, a par-67 layout that offers a less intense but still worthwhile day out, is available at around £120 per player as an indicative figure. Full prepayment is required and fees are non-refundable.

Caddie hire is available; confirm pricing with the club. Bring cash for the caddie as there is no ATM on site. A handicap certificate is required for the Championship Links, so make sure yours is current and to hand.

## Ardglass Golf Club

Ardglass sits around 30 minutes from Newcastle by car, perched on clifftops above the Irish Sea. The club was founded in 1896 and plays to a par-70 over 6,216 yards. The clubhouse incorporates a 15th-century castle structure, which gives it a setting unlike any other in Irish golf.

This is the sort of course that would headline a lesser county. The clifftop holes are exposed and unforgiving in a wind, but the views are substantial compensation. It is open to visitors, though advance booking is advised.

Indicative green fees are in the region of £115 on a weekday and £135 at weekends in high season, though these figures vary by source and season. Confirm current rates directly with the club before travelling.

## Kirkistown Castle Golf Club

Kirkistown Castle, on the Ards Peninsula, is around 45 minutes from Belfast and represents something close to the hidden gem of County Down's golf circuit. Founded in 1902 with a layout reworked by James Braid in 1934, it is the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland, playing to a par-69 over 6,167 yards on quick-draining sandy links that are rarely closed even through winter.

The course has a character all its own. The first and 18th holes share a fairway, and the distinctive 'Dip' near the first tee is a legacy of wartime airfield construction on the site. The signature hole is the par-4 10th, known as Long Reach, a 435-yard brute with an elevated plateau green that is widely regarded as one of the hardest par-4s in Irish golf.

What makes Kirkistown particularly appealing for a multi-day circuit is the green fee. Indicative rates are around £35 midweek and £40 at weekends, though again, verify directly with the club. The course has previously been recognised as Best Value Golf Experience in Ulster, and it is not hard to see why.

## Kilkeel: The Parkland Option

For a contrast to three consecutive links days, Kilkeel Golf Club at Mourne Park offers an 18-hole parkland course set within the Kilmorey Estate at the foot of the Mournes. It plays to a par-72 over 6,615 yards through mature woodland and rhododendron, and hosted (alongside Royal County Down) the 1999 British Amateur. Indicative green fees are roughly £35 to £50 depending on day and source; confirm directly before travelling.

## Practical Notes for the Circuit

Wind is a constant at all the coastal courses, and Mourne mist descends quickly regardless of the morning forecast. A full waterproof suit is not optional gear here, it is core kit. Pack it and do not leave it in the hire car.

For accommodation, Newcastle is the obvious base, with the Slieve Donard Resort close to the RCD gates and smaller options such as the Burrendale Hotel about a mile away. Belfast works as an alternative hub if you prefer city amenities and are comfortable with the 45-minute drive to RCD each morning.

---

## The Honest Takeaway

The case for spending three or four days on County Down's golf circuit rather than a single pilgrimage to Royal County Down is straightforward. You get more golf, more variety, more scenery, and your cost per round drops considerably once Kirkistown and Ardglass are in the schedule alongside RCD. The logistics are manageable from anywhere in mainland UK, particularly if you use the ferry and bring your own clubs. Book Royal County Down early, everything else falls into place around it.
