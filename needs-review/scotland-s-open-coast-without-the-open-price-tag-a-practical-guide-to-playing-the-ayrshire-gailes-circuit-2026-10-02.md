Most golfers planning an Ayrshire trip build their itinerary around the big three: Royal Troon, Prestwick, and Turnberry. That is entirely understandable. What tends to happen, though, is that the marquee green fees eat the budget, the advance booking stress eats the enjoyment, and a cluster of genuinely excellent links courses within twenty minutes of each other gets ignored entirely.

This post makes the case for the Ayrshire Gailes circuit: Western Gailes, Glasgow Golf Club's Gailes Links, Dundonald Links, Kilmarnock Barassie, West Kilbride, and Prestwick St Nicholas. Treat these as the main event, or use them as relief rounds alongside the headline venues. Either way, they deserve your attention.

## Why Troon Makes Sense as a Base

Royal Troon sits in the town itself, Prestwick is ten minutes south, and Western Gailes is ten minutes north. Turnberry is around forty minutes further south if you want it. Staying in or near Troon means you can drive to every course on this list without it becoming a half-day exercise in itself.

A practical note before the marquee question comes up: Royal Troon is weekday-only for visitors, requires significant advance booking, and carries green fees to match its status. If the Open venues are on your list, plan those slots separately and well ahead. The courses below are generally easier to access and considerably easier on the wallet.

## The Courses Worth Your Time

### Western Gailes

This is the one that serious links golfers tend to mention first when the conversation turns to Ayrshire's less-visited courses. Founded in 1897 and regularly used as an Open Qualifying venue, it occupies a narrow strip of duneland between the Firth of Clyde and the Ayrshire Coast Line railway. That setting alone tells you what kind of golf you are in for: exposed, traditional, uncompromising.

Visitor access is straightforward by Scottish links standards. A handicap certificate is required. Published green fees for 2026 run from £95 on a winter weekday up to £335 for a summer Saturday, with soup and sandwiches included on weekday peak-season rounds. If you are prepared to travel mid-week and out of high summer, this is a serious course at a manageable price.

Worth noting: the Ayrshire Coast Line runs directly alongside the course, which makes a day trip from Glasgow genuinely practical if you are not staying locally.

### Glasgow Golf Club (Gailes Links)

Glasgow Golf Club is one of the oldest clubs in the world, founded in 1787. Its Gailes Links course opened in 1892, designed by Willie Park Jnr, and plays as a par 71 over 6,903 yards. The quality of the design shows; this is not a course that was built to be convenient.

Visitor days are restricted, so contact the club directly before making plans around it. A handicap certificate is required. Green fees in 2026 run from £95 in winter to £180 in summer, figures checked in June 2026. Getting this one on the schedule takes a bit of planning, but it rounds out the Gailes cluster nicely.

### Dundonald Links

The newest course in this group, Dundonald has hosted the Scottish Open and plays as a modern resort links with proper championship credentials. It is bookable online, which makes logistics simpler than some of the member-focused clubs nearby. Green fees in 2026 run from around £95 to £195 per round depending on season and timing.

### Kilmarnock Barassie

Twenty-seven holes of proper links golf just outside Troon, and part of the Ayrshire Golf Pass alongside Irvine Golf Club, Prestwick St Nicholas, and West Kilbride. If you are playing multiple courses from this list, the pass is worth investigating as a way to structure the trip.

### West Kilbride

West Kilbride plays 6,523 yards from the championship tees and offers panoramic views across the Firth of Clyde to Arran on a clear day. It has hosted the R&A Seniors Home Internationals, the Scottish Boys' Championship, and the Scottish PGA Championship, which gives you a reasonable measure of what the course is capable of demanding.

Visitors are welcome by prior arrangement, with weekday access and weekend availability after 3pm. Green fees for 2026 run from £95 to £125 for one to three players in April and October, rising to £125 to £165 in the May to September peak. Worth contacting ahead rather than assuming walk-up availability.

### Prestwick St Nicholas

This is the budget-friendly anchor of the circuit. Prestwick St Nicholas sits directly opposite championship Prestwick and shares something of the same Old Tom Morris-era heritage, without the same price tag. Reported green fees sit in the £90 to £130 range, and it is part of the Ayrshire Golf Pass. If you want a sense of Prestwick's golfing history without the full outlay, this is worth a few hours.

## Package Options Worth Knowing About

Two multi-course packages cover much of this ground:

The **Gailes Golf Experience** combines Dundonald Links, Gailes Links, and Western Gailes. 2026 pricing runs from £215 per person in March up to around £430 or above in the May to September window for all three rounds.

The **Ayrshire Links Experience** covers Dundonald Links, Barassie Links, and Irvine Golf Club at similar 2026 pricing.

If you are building a dedicated trip around this area, either package simplifies the booking process and offers reasonable value for what you get.

## A Practical Four-Day Sketch

A workable approach for a four-day visit from Troon:

- Day one: Arrive, play the Darley municipal course in Troon (council-run links, reported at £20 to £28) to shake off travel and get a feel for links conditions.
- Day two: Western Gailes (book ahead, mid-week if possible).
- Day three: Gailes Links and Prestwick St Nicholas, or pick up the Ayrshire Golf Pass courses.
- Day four: Dundonald Links, then drive south to Turnberry or Prestwick if the marquee venues are on the list.

Adjust based on which clubs have availability and whether you have booked through one of the experience packages.

## The Honest Case for the Circuit

None of these courses are secrets exactly, but they are systematically overlooked by golfers who arrive in Ayrshire with a Troon tee time booked months in advance and not much else confirmed. The courses around Gailes and the southern stretch of the coast offer genuine championship links golf, reasonable visitor access, and in most cases the chance to actually get a tee time without engineering it like a military operation.

The marquee venues are worth playing if they are on your list. But for a trip built around the golf rather than the postcards, the Ayrshire Gailes circuit is where the real value sits.
