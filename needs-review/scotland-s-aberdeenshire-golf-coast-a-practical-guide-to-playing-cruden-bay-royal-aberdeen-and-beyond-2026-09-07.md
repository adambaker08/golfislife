Most Scottish golf trips anchor themselves at St Andrews, with maybe a swing up to Royal Dornoch for the dedicated pilgrim. The stretch in between, the Aberdeenshire coastline running north from Aberdeen up to Fraserburgh, gets quietly passed over. That's a mistake worth correcting. This is some of the finest links golf on the planet, and for now it remains pleasingly uncrowded compared to Fife.

This guide covers a practical two-to-three day circuit along the Aberdeenshire golf coast, taking in the headline names and a few affordable additions, with honest notes on access, sequencing, and what to expect from the courses themselves.

## Getting There and Getting Around

Aberdeen Airport sits about seven miles northwest of the city centre, with direct flights from most major UK airports. Edinburgh by road via the A90 takes roughly two and a half hours; the train runs to a similar schedule. Once you're there, a hire car is essential. The courses are spread across 50-plus miles of coast and public transport won't join the dots.

Royal Aberdeen is around 20 minutes from the airport. Cruden Bay is 23 miles north of the city on the A90 and A975, roughly 35 minutes further. That geography shapes the logical itinerary.

## The Courses

### Cruden Bay

This is the one that tends to stop people mid-sentence when they describe it. Old Tom Morris laid out the original in 1899; Tom Simpson redesigned it in 1926, and what Simpson created is a course that moves through the dunes rather than around them. Fairways vanish over ridges, greens sit in natural amphitheatres, and the wind off the North Sea can fundamentally change how any given hole plays.

Visitors are welcome seven days a week, though weekends are restricted to afternoon tee times. Booking opens up to 12 months ahead via the club's own website, and you'll want to use that window for peak season. Green fees vary by season: expect to pay somewhere in the region of £200 or more in peak summer, with meaningfully lower rates in shoulder and winter periods. The club's website will have current pricing; given that figures vary slightly between sources, it's worth checking directly before you book rather than relying on third-party estimates.

Caddies can be arranged at Cruden Bay and are worth considering, particularly on a first visit. The blind shots are numerous and the club selection into the wind takes local knowledge.

After your round, Slains Castle is a five-minute drive away. Roofless, dramatic, and sitting right on the cliff edge, it's an easy addition and a good excuse to stretch your legs before the drive back.

### Royal Aberdeen (Balgownie)

Founded in 1780 as the Society of Golfers at Aberdeen, this is one of the oldest clubs in the world. The Balgownie course is a traditional out-and-back links on the North Sea shore, originally designed by the Simpson Brothers and later extended and re-bunkered by James Braid.

It plays differently from Cruden Bay but is every bit as serious a test. The front nine heads north through towering dunes; the back nine returns south with the wind (usually) at a different angle. Green fees sit at a premium level in line with its status. Check current rates directly with the club.

Caddies are available here too, and the same logic applies: if it's your first visit, the blind approach shots will reward local guidance.

### Murcar Links

A ten-minute drive from Royal Aberdeen, Murcar makes a natural partner on the same day. Established in 1909, it's built across natural dunes with gorse, heather, elevated tees, and views out to the North Sea. It consistently ranks among Scotland's better links and tends to be quieter than its more famous neighbour. Green fees are generally more accessible than Royal Aberdeen, making it a good option if you want to play 36 holes without the budget taking a significant second hit.

### Trump International Golf Links (Menie Estate)

The Old Course here was designed by Martin Hawtree and opened in 2012, stretching to 7,428 yards across the Menie Estate's spectacular dune system. A second course opened in July 2025, and the development has been picking up significant recognition in the golf world. The site hosts DP World Tour events from 2025 onwards.

It divides opinion as a venue for reasons unrelated to the golf, but the courses themselves are worth knowing about if you're building a full Aberdeenshire circuit.

## The Supporting Acts

**Royal Tarlair (Macduff)** is about 55 minutes north of Aberdeen, which puts it at the edge of a comfortable day trip, but the 13th hole alone has a strong reputation and green fees are around £25, making it one of the best-value rounds in this part of Scotland. Worth considering if you're extending to a third day.

**Fraserburgh Golf Club** was founded in 1777, with the present course opened in 1891 and reshaped by James Braid in 1922. That history puts it among Scotland's oldest clubs, and it deserves more recognition than it generally gets.

**Peterhead Craigewan Links** sits on the coast just south of Fraserburgh and tends to fly under the radar entirely. It's a genuine hidden gem for anyone willing to add an extra stop.

**Newburgh-on-Ythan** is a short drive south of Aberdeen and offers another affordable coastal round if you want to bookend a trip.

## A Suggested Itinerary

- **Day 1:** Drive up to Cruden Bay for a morning or afternoon round depending on your arrival. Visit Slains Castle. Stay overnight in the village (the Kilmarnock Arms is the well-known option locally) or head back to Aberdeen.
- **Day 2:** Royal Aberdeen in the morning, then Murcar Links or Trump International in the afternoon. City hotel in Aberdeen works well as a base.
- **Day 3:** Royal Tarlair or Fraserburgh before heading south. This adds mileage but rounds out the trip if you have the time.

## Practical Notes

The weather on this coast shifts quickly and doesn't always telegraph its intentions. Waterproofs, a warm mid-layer, and a windproof jacket are not optional. Even in July you'll be glad of them by the back nine.

Non-golfing companions have options too: the Speyside whisky trail is within reach for a day off, and the coastal walks between courses are genuinely good.

## The Honest Case for Aberdeenshire

This part of Scotland doesn't market itself as aggressively as St Andrews, and the courses don't always appear in the headline conversations. That's precisely the point. Top100golfcourses.com noted in an early 2026 update that Aberdeenshire's coastline offers some of the finest seaside golf anywhere, much of it still refreshingly free of the crowds that have overtaken more famous destinations. That window won't stay open indefinitely. A dedicated two or three days here, sequenced properly, stacks up against just about any links trip in Britain.
