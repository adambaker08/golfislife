Ask most visiting golfers where they would go for a dedicated golf trip in England and you will hear the same answers: Sandwich, Sunningdale, Swinley Forest, maybe a run up to Northumberland. Yorkshire rarely makes the shortlist. That is a genuine oversight, because this county holds a concentration of golden-age design, Ryder Cup history, and terrain variety that is hard to match anywhere else in England. This guide makes the case for Yorkshire as a golf trip destination and gives you a practical framework for actually putting one together.

## Three Ryder Cup Venues in One County

The hook is almost absurdly strong. Yorkshire has hosted the Ryder Cup at three separate venues: Moortown in 1929, Ganton in 1949, and Lindrick in 1957. The Lindrick match is particularly significant — Great Britain won that contest, the first time the home side had won in 24 years. That kind of history tends to be celebrated loudly; somehow, Yorkshire's version of it stays relatively quiet.

All three courses remain in excellent condition and are open, with varying degrees of accessibility, to visiting golfers.

## The MacKenzie Thread

If the Ryder Cup history is the hook, the Alister MacKenzie connection is the thread that runs through the whole trip. MacKenzie, born in Leeds, began his design career in the county, and several of his courses sit within a few miles of each other in the north Leeds suburbs.

**Alwoodley** was MacKenzie's first design, laid out in 1907 on Wigton Moor. It is a heathland-moorland hybrid and, by most accounts, a very fine one. Bunkered has noted that the 10th hole is said to have influenced the 13th at Augusta National. Access here is the most restricted of the Leeds cluster: visitors need to arrange their round via member sponsorship and should contact the club well in advance. It is not impossible to get on, but you cannot simply ring up and book a tee time.

**Moortown**, half a mile away, is the 1929 Ryder Cup venue and is generally more accessible to visiting parties. Classic moorland character: heather, birch, peaty turf underfoot. If Alwoodley is the pilgrimage, Moortown is the more practical companion round.

**Sand Moor**, also a MacKenzie design (laid out 1921, opened 1923), completes a triumvirate within the same north Leeds area. It is generally regarded as the value option among the cluster without being a poor relation.

The proximity of these three courses is unusual. Playing all of them in a long weekend is entirely feasible.

## Ganton: The Flagship

Ganton, near Scarborough, is listed as the top-ranked course in Yorkshire and it earns that position honestly. Par 72, just under 7,000 yards, with a character best described as heathland-inland-links: gorse, sandy subsoil, and firm turf that rewards proper links thinking rather than aerial target golf.

The roll call of people who have shaped it over the decades is extraordinary: Harry Vardon (who was club professional when he won his first Open Championship in 1896), James Braid, JH Taylor, Harry Colt, and Alister MacKenzie have all had a hand in it. It has also hosted the Walker Cup (2003) and the Curtis Cup (2000). Booking ahead is essential; Ganton is not a course you drop into on spec.

## Other Courses Worth Your Attention

- **Hallamshire, Sheffield.** Laid out on former Duke of Norfolk estate land in 1897, this is Matt Fitzpatrick's home club. It featured in the NCG Top 100s Tour in April 2026 alongside Lindrick, a pairing that signals the South Yorkshire leg of a Yorkshire trip has real substance.

- **Fulford, York.** Well set up for society and group visits, strong practice facilities, parkland in character. It has its own footnote in golf history: Bernhard Langer's tree-climbing incident during the 1981 Benson and Hedges International happened here.

- **York Golf Club.** A JH Taylor design north of the city, flat heathland lined by mature woodland. A quieter option that fits naturally into any York-based leg of a trip.

- **Cleveland Golf Club.** Founded in 1887, the oldest club in the Yorkshire Union, sitting on the North Yorkshire coast with views across the North Sea. Links in character and often overlooked even within Yorkshire itself.

- **Pannal (Harrogate) and Ilkley** also feature in the county's top ten and make natural additions if you are basing yourself in the Harrogate or Bradford area.

## Planning the Itinerary

Yorkshire is a large county and trying to see all of it in one trip will leave you driving more than playing. The sensible approach is to build sub-itineraries around natural clusters.

**The Leeds cluster.** Alwoodley, Moortown, Sand Moor, and (if you want to add a fourth) Headingley, all sitting within a compact area north of the city. Leeds makes a practical base; Harrogate is elegant and barely further from the courses.

**The South Yorkshire leg.** Lindrick and Hallamshire, both with Ryder Cup or near-Ryder-Cup credentials. Sheffield as a base keeps travel short.

**Coast and Ganton.** Ganton paired with Cleveland Golf Club, using Scarborough or the surrounding area for accommodation. Ganton's own Dormy House is an option worth investigating.

**The stay-and-play packages.** Alwoodley's website (as of early 2026) lists two packages worth knowing about. A joint Alwoodley and Moortown stay-and-play starts from approximately £339 per person sharing. A Friday-Saturday combination of Alwoodley and Ganton, with accommodation at The Village Hotel Leeds North or Ganton's Dormy House, starts from approximately £419 per person sharing. Both figures are from the club's own materials for the 2026 season and are subject to change, so treat them as a planning guide rather than a fixed quote and confirm current pricing when you book.

## The Practical Realities

A few things to know before you go:

Alwoodley requires member sponsorship for visitor access. Contact the club directly and give plenty of lead time.

Ganton, Moortown, Fulford, and most others are more straightforwardly bookable, but visitor slots at the top courses fill up, especially for weekend rounds.

The Leeds cluster and Harrogate-area courses are within easy reach of Leeds Bradford Airport. Ganton is roughly 45 minutes from York by road. Hallamshire and Lindrick sit in a different direction entirely, so combining them with the Leeds cluster means either a longer base or a longer drive.

## The Honest Takeaway

Yorkshire does not shout about itself the way the great Scottish or Irish golf destinations do, and the visitor infrastructure is not as polished as you get at some of the more deliberately tourist-facing regions. What it offers instead is serious golf, serious history, and the odd satisfaction of playing courses that many regular golfers have never got around to visiting. The MacKenzie legacy alone would justify the trip. The Ryder Cup venues make it straightforward to defend. The terrain variety means you can build several quite different itineraries without repeating yourself. It rewards the golfer who does a bit of planning and books ahead.
