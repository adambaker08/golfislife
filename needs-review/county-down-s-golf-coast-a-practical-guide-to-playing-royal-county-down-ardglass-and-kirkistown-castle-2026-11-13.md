## Why County Down Deserves More Than One Round

There is a version of a County Down golf trip that goes: fly to Belfast, play Royal County Down, fly home. It is entirely understandable. When Golf Digest ranks a course number one in the world on its 2024 list, it tends to dominate the conversation.

But County Down's golf coast is broader than a single club, and treating it as a one-round pilgrimage means missing two courses that each have something genuinely distinctive to offer: Ardglass, with its cliffside opening holes and a clubhouse older than most nations, and Kirkistown Castle, the most easterly links on the island of Ireland and arguably the best-value round in the county. A few days here, spread across all three, makes for a proper golf circuit rather than a tick-box trip.

This guide covers the practical logistics: access rules, green fees (where confirmed), how to get there from mainland UK, and where to base yourself.

---

## Royal County Down: The World Number One

Royal County Down (RCD) sits in Newcastle, tucked between the Mourne Mountains and Dundrum Bay. Founded in 1889 and shaped almost entirely from natural duneland, it is one of those courses where the setting does half the work before you have even teed up. The Championship Links is par 71, plays to a slope rating of 142 from the blue tees, and has hosted the Walker Cup, multiple Irish Opens and the Senior British Open. Buggies are not permitted.

### Visitor access

Access for visiting golfers is restricted. As of 2026, the Championship Links is open to visitors on Mondays, Tuesdays and Fridays (all day), Thursday mornings, and Sunday afternoons. Wednesdays and Saturdays are closed to visitors entirely. Advance booking is essential and the club operates a waitlist system for peak dates. Book early.

The club also has the Annesley Links, a second 18-hole course that is considerably more affordable and also more accessible for visitors.

### Green fees

Championship green fees are premium and vary by season and tee time. Sources differ enough that it would be misleading to quote a single figure here. Check current rates directly at royalcountydown.org before planning your budget. Annesley Link fees are substantially lower and worth considering if you want a second day on-site without the full outlay.

## Ardglass: Cliffside Links and the World's Oldest Golf Clubhouse

About 20 miles up the coast from Newcastle, Ardglass Golf Club occupies a clifftop setting above the Irish Sea. Founded in 1896, the course is par 70 and plays to 5,814 yards. It is a compact, characterful links where the first five holes run along the cliffs, with the sea close enough that you are very aware of it.

The clubhouse is worth a mention on its own terms. The building is a fortified warehouse dating to no later than 1405 and is reputedly the oldest building in continuous use as a golf clubhouse in the world. Knowing that tends to make the post-round pint feel slightly more significant.

Ardglass was listed in Irish Golfer's top 50 Irish courses for 2026, which feels about right for a course this characterful at this price point.

### Green fees and access

The official 2026 green fees from the club's own website are:

- **High season (18 holes):** £250 | **Daily rate:** £375
- **Shoulder (April):** £125
- **Low season (November, February–March):** £90

Golf Ireland members pay lower rates. Visitors are welcome most days, but Saturday mornings and Sunday mornings are reserved for members. Wednesdays and busy weekends are best avoided.

## Kirkistown Castle: The Underrated One

Kirkistown Castle Golf Club sits on the Ards Peninsula at Cloughey, roughly 45 minutes by road from Belfast. Founded in 1902 and visited by designer James Braid in 1934, it is an 18-hole links playing to around 6,167 yards at par 69/70.

A few things make Kirkistown worth knowing about. It is the most easterly 18-hole course on the island of Ireland. It sits in the lowest rainfall area of Northern Ireland and drains freely from its sandy base, which means it tends to play well year-round when other courses are carrying more moisture. And it is significantly more affordable than either RCD or Ardglass.

The standout hole is the par-4 10th, a 435-yard test with a reputation as one of the hardest par 4s in Irish golf. If you are building a County Down itinerary on a range of budgets or simply want to play something a bit different from the premium marquee options, Kirkistown fits neatly.

Green fees are modest, with multiple sources suggesting around £40 per round, but confirm directly with the club before travelling as this figure may not reflect current pricing. Visitors are welcome every day; advance booking is advised.

## Getting There from Mainland UK

A car is effectively necessary for County Down. The courses are spread along the coast and peninsula and public transport does not connect them usefully.

**By air:** Belfast City Airport is the closest to County Down and the most convenient. Belfast International is the alternative if flights suit better.

**By ferry:** This is worth considering, particularly if you want to bring your own clubs without airline baggage costs. Stena Line runs Cairnryan (southwest Scotland) to Belfast in around 2 hours 15 minutes, and also an overnight Liverpool to Belfast sailing of around 8 hours. P&O runs Cairnryan to Larne in approximately 2 hours. Drive-on access with golf bags in the boot removes the usual airline fumble.

## Where to Stay

Newcastle is the natural base for Royal County Down. The Slieve Donard Resort is immediately adjacent to the first tee. The Burrendale Hotel is about a mile from the club's entrance and tends to be a quieter option.

If you are splitting the trip across the peninsula to include Kirkistown, it may be worth looking at accommodation around Portaferry or Strangford rather than driving back to Newcastle each evening.

---

## Other Courses Worth Knowing

County Down has more golf than these three. Warrenpoint Golf Club is a parkland course from 1893 on the southern edge of the county, and Clandeboye Golf Club near Conlig offers 36 holes of parkland golf. Both suit a mixed trip where not everyone in the group wants links every day.

## The Honest Takeaway

The case for a County Down golf circuit is straightforward: you get the world's top-ranked course, a genuinely historic cliffside links at Ardglass, and a low-key gem on the Ards Peninsula, all within a relatively compact area and with ferry options that make it easy to bring your own clubs. Three or four days covers it properly. One day does not.
